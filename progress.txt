=====================================================================
 THESIS PROGRESS UPDATE
 Multi-drone cooperative cable-suspended payload transport
 (ROS 2 + Gazebo/Ignition, acados nonlinear MPC)
=====================================================================
Date: 2026-07-27

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ONE-LINE SUMMARY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
A fleet of quadrotors cooperatively carries a cable-suspended payload
along prescribed trajectories. Drones can LEAVE the fleet mid-flight
(detach) and JOIN it mid-flight (attach) while the payload keeps
flying. Mid-flight ATTACH is the novel contribution of the thesis.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CONTROL METHODS DEVELOPED
---------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) CENTRALIZED MPC METHOD  [working in sim]
    - Nonlinear model-predictive control built on the acados OCP
      solver. A central planner generates the payload trajectory;
      a cable-aware MPC tracker flies each drone so the load follows
      it. Handles the coupled quad + cable + payload dynamics.
    - Status: flies the 2-4 drone fleet along the trajectory set
      (hover, straight-line, circle/orbit) with the load stable.
    - Role: the baseline / reference controller and the takeoff
      (ground break-off) stage.

(b) DECENTRALIZED DISSIPATIVE METHOD  [working in sim]
    - A virtual-node spring-damper "network" that generates each
      drone's flight reference in a decentralized way. It is a
      faithful port of Quan et al., "Self-Organizing Aerial Swarm
      Robotics: A Table-Mechanics-Inspired Approach," adapted to the
      rigid-cable, mocap-pinned payload here.
    - Key property: a member joining or leaving is the network's
      NATIVE operating mode -- no solver switch, no hand-designed
      redistribution. When a drone leaves, the survivors re-settle and
      their share of the load rises automatically; when one joins, the
      reverse happens.
    - Verified OFFLINE first: a numpy closed-loop test harness drives
      the real controller against a small multibody plant (drones +
      cables + payload + ground contact) and asserts, as a pass/fail
      gate, that the load stays aloft, tensions redistribute correctly,
      and the formation re-settles through each detach/attach event.
      This gate is run before every Gazebo test.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DETACHING  [working & verified in sim -- this is the demo video]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    - A tethered drone can leave the fleet mid-flight (via an RViz
      DETACH button). It releases, flies clear, and the remaining
      drones re-settle around the payload with the load held level and
      the trajectory continuing uninterrupted.
    - Confirmed in simulation with both the MPC and the dissipative
      method. The fleet redistributes to even spacing after a detach
      (e.g. 4->3->2), the per-drone load share rises correctly, and a
      LAND command safely lands both the attached and detached drones.
    - This is the mechanism I will show in the video.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ATTACHING  [in progress -- the thesis novelty]
---------------------------------------------------------------------
    - Goal: a free drone carrying a swung electromagnet flies to the
      already-suspended payload, welds onto it mid-flight, and JOINS
      the fleet so the load continues with one more drone. This is the
      reverse of detach and, to my knowledge, the novel contribution.
    - Pipeline integrated end-to-end in simulation:
        * an approach MPC flies the free drone to the payload;
        * the electromagnet welds it on (Gazebo detachable joint),
          triggered by an RViz ATTACH button;
        * motor authority hands over from the approach controller to
          the fleet's tracker at the weld;
        * the drone becomes node 4 of the dissipative network.
    - Working today: the drone approaches, welds, and joins as a
      central (vertical) lifter -- stable and tilt-free.
    - Current focus: making the fleet visibly RECONFIGURE after the
      newcomer joins (redistribute the load rather than just add a
      central lifter). The physics challenge: three tethers are fixed
      at 120-degree attach points, so the four drones cannot form an
      evenly-spaced ring under equal load sharing. The solution being
      implemented is UNEQUAL (moment-balanced) force sharing: the
      network solves for per-drone tensions from a full 6-DOF
      force/moment balance at the true attach geometry, so an
      asymmetric four-drone arrangement holds the payload level. This
      is passing the offline verification gate; sim tuning is ongoing.
