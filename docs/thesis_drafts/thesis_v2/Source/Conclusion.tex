This thesis set out to test whether a centralised controller can carry a cable-suspended payload
through the departure and the mid-flight arrival of individual UAVs, with a centralised load--cable
planner based on \cite{tudelft} and a cable-aware tracking MPC per UAV, flown on Betaflight UAVs over
their radio links with motion capture as the only sensing.

\paragraph{Q1: carrying with a fixed team.} On hardware, three and then four UAVs carried the 0.86\,kg ring
to 1.0\,m, around a circle and through a figure-eight, within 3\,cm of the path on average once the
trackers used the rod pull they measure. The remaining gaps to simulation, a low hover and a steady
horizontal offset, have identified causes or candidate causes.

\paragraph{Q2: steady accuracy.} Steady payload errors in the re-planned cascade come from sources that look
alike at the payload (the tracker's relaxed initial condition, a breakaway pull cap held for the whole flight,
model errors and external forces), and each needed a different remedy. Integral action in the planner's model
removed the steady error from external forces in all three axes in simulation, and on the rig the trackers'
measured rod pull removed most of the force the integral had been learning. A per-UAV thrust error is a moment
that only a per-UAV correction removes.

\paragraph{RQ1a: leave.} Reconfiguration can be handled by resizing the planner's optimal control problem,
because the attachment geometry is a runtime parameter. In simulation a detach this way left the payload level
within seconds, and on the rig it held the ring in all four flights, announced and unannounced, with peak tilts
of 6.5--17.8$^\circ$.

\paragraph{Value added.} What is new is a centralised way to change the team under a carried payload, flown
on hardware. A detach or an attach is a switch to a pre-built solver with the attachment geometry as a runtime
parameter, so in the first tick after the event the planner solves for the load distribution of the team it
actually has, instead of leaving the survivors to find it from the motion that follows. On the rig this carried
the ring on through four detaches, announced and unannounced, in hover and on a circle, a transient that none of
the closest works reports (Table~\ref{tab:lit_compare}). Two further results transfer beyond this rig: a
geometric condition, checkable before a flight, for whether a layout can lose a given UAV and keep the payload
level, and a map of the steady payload errors of a re-planned cascade, each with its signature at the payload and
its remedy (Table~\ref{tab:error_sources}). They are of use to groups that build cooperative transport on
commercial UAVs, where the onboard force control of \cite{tudelft} is not available: a ground-side cascade on
unmodified Betaflight UAVs, with motion capture as its only sensor, carried a rigid-rod payload within 3\,cm of its
path once each tracker measured its rod's pull. They are also of use to work on fault tolerance and on teams whose
members are replaced in flight. The detach on the rig, with the twin alongside, is the core of a publishable result
on reconfiguring cooperative transport. What it still lacks is scale, repeats and the other half: a 0.86\,kg ring
at up to 0.25\,m/s in a motion-capture room, one rig run per condition, and the attach, which no reviewed work has
flown and which this work has so far flown only in the twin.

\paragraph{Future work.} The immediate steps are the attach on the rig and then the full rejoin with
the collaborator's hardware, the model integral through a detach on the rig, and a check of the
motion-capture calibration for the horizontal pull. Beyond this thesis: a model of slack cables,
so the planner can take over before the rods are taut; force sensing at the UAVs, the onboard
equivalent of \cite{tudelft}'s incremental inversion, to remove per-UAV thrust errors; outdoor
flight without motion capture; and larger teams, for which a pre-built solver per team size becomes
a memory and build-time cost.
