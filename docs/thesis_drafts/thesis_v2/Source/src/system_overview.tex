\subsection{System and platform}
\label{sec:overview}

\subsubsection{Nodes and safety}

All control runs centrally, on one ground computer under ROS~2 (Robot Operating System~2). Motion
capture (OptiTrack) is the only sensing: it gives the pose of each UAV and of the payload, and nothing
on the payload is instrumented. Each UAV's flight controller runs Betaflight in rate mode, receives a
collective throttle and three body-rate stick commands over its own ExpressLRS (ELRS) radio link, and
closes only its inner rate loop. Figure~\ref{fig:nodes} shows the nodes and what they exchange.

The system has three layers.
\begin{description}
    \item[Planner.] The centralised load--cable MPC of Section~\ref{sec:controller} plans the motion of
    the payload and of every rod at 10\,Hz and publishes each UAV's reference over the horizon. The
    dissipative network of Section~\ref{sec:dissipative} can replace it in flight; both publish the
    same reference format, so the trackers do not know which one is active.
    \item[Trackers.] One tracking MPC per UAV at 50\,Hz turns its UAV's part of the plan into the radio
    commands.
    \item[Fleet manager.] It sequences ARM, TAKEOFF (the climb, the rod sweep to the working elevation
    and the lift), the trajectory, LAND, and the reconfiguration events DETACH and ATTACH
    (Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}).
\end{description}
\paragraph{Safety.} Every tracker checks its own UAV against a flight envelope (tilt and payload tilt
both 60$^\circ$, speed 3\,m/s) and checks that its motion-capture pose (0.25\,s) and the planner's
reference (1\,s) are fresh. Any violation disarms the whole fleet, not the one UAV: a single UAV losing
thrust on a shared rigid payload drags the others with it. The exceptions are UAVs that are not on the payload: a fault on a newcomer
that is not yet welded disarms the newcomer alone and lands the fleet, and a UAV that has left the payload is
pushed down to its resting height and disarmed alone (Figure~\ref{fig:disarm_lanes}). LAND lands every UAV,
whatever it is doing. There is no geofence; the operator holds a
kill switch. On the rig each UAV must confirm that its flight controller is armed before the fleet
takes off, and a UAV that disarms between ARM and TAKEOFF grounds the fleet and TAKEOFF is refused,
since three UAVs lifting a ring sized for four would drag it. On the rig the planner starts no new
iteration after 60\,ms, and a plan that has not converged by then is replaced by the previous one, shifted
by a tick.

\subsubsection{Rig hardware}
\label{sec:hardware}

\paragraph{UAVs.} Four UAVs run Betaflight in rate mode on six-cell batteries. Each has its own
ELRS receiver and a ground transmitter on a USB serial port, so every UAV is commanded on its own link
at 100\,Hz: the throttle, three rate sticks, the arm switch and an auxiliary channel that switches the
UAV's magnet, held on from start-up so that a quiet link cannot drop the payload. The flight
controller's telemetry (battery voltage, flight mode, link quality) comes back on the same link. As
flown, each UAV weighs 0.55\,kg: an airframe and battery of 0.475\,kg, plus its rod and magnet.

\paragraph{Rods, payload and motion capture.} Each UAV carries a rigid rod of 0.55\,m and 0.075\,kg with
a ball joint at both ends; the upper joint sits 4\,cm below the UAV's centre. The payload is a 0.86\,kg
ring of 0.51\,m diameter and 40\,mm thickness, with twelve magnet plates every 30$^\circ$ on its top face
at a radius of 0.225\,m. Each rod ends in an electromagnet that holds on a plate with 10--12\,N and
releases under a jerk. A layout is named by its plate numbers, anticlockwise from plate 0 on the ring's
$+x$ axis (Figure~\ref{fig:geometry}); Figure~\ref{fig:rig_photo} shows the rig. OptiTrack tracks one
rigid body per UAV and one for the ring. Its poses are the only state measurement: the velocities and
body rates of the UAVs and of the ring are differentiated from them.
