\section{Introduction}

An unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) is an aircraft with no onboard pilot, flown autonomously or by remote control. A quadcopter is a UAV with four horizontal rotors; it is underactuated, with four control inputs for six degrees of freedom \cite{kotaruDifferentialflatnessControlQuadrotors2018}. In this thesis, UAV means a quadcopter. Low-cost microcontrollers and sensors have taken UAVs from hobby and videography uses to package delivery, search and rescue, construction and agriculture \cite{kardaszDronesPossibilitiesTheir2016, olleroPresentFutureAerial2022}, helped by their agility, vertical takeoff and landing, and ability to operate in confined or GPS-denied spaces. Many of these tasks require carrying a payload \cite{thusooQUADROTORSPRESENTERA2021, choiOverviewDroneApplications2023}, and UAV payload transport has received significant research attention over the past two decades.

A single UAV's thrust-to-weight ratio limits the payload it can carry, and a suspended load adds pendulum-like swing that can destabilise the vehicle and costs control effort to reject \cite{geronelDynamicResponsesDue2023}. In cooperative multi-UAV transport several UAVs lift one load \cite{Carney_2022}. This raises the liftable mass, constrains the load's swing through several attachment points, can give full six-degree-of-freedom control of the payload's pose, and avoids building one large vehicle \cite{tudelft, mohammadiDecentralizedMotionControl2018}.

The payload is commonly attached through flexible cables, which are mechanically simple and safer in a collision than rigid links. Cable tension must stay positive for a cable to stay taut, and in a team each UAV's share of it must be allocated so that no vehicle is overloaded; formation constraints and inter-UAV collision avoidance complicate the problem further \cite{kotaruDynamicsControlQuadrotor2017}.

A large body of work addresses trajectory tracking, formation control, tension allocation and disturbance rejection for fixed teams \cite{wangRobustCooperativeTransportation2024, gimenezMultiobjectiveControlCooperative2018, wahbaKinodynamicMotionPlanning2024}. Most of it is validated in simulation; fewer papers show hardware results, and very few fly outdoors, where most applications are \cite{estevezReviewAerialTransportation2024}.

Changes in team composition during a mission have received much less attention than disturbances such as wind and payload uncertainty. In this thesis, reconfiguration means a change of team membership, a UAV leaving or joining while the payload is carried; it is distinct from a fixed team changing its formation, as when a team reshapes itself to pass a doorway \cite{jackson2020scalable}. A UAV leaving has been flown: Quan et al.\ \cite{quan2025swarm} release one of four UAVs from a 4\,kg load in flight and the other three rebalance their cable tensions, Zeng et al.\ \cite{zeng2025marl} keep a 1.4\,kg load under control with two of three MAVs after the third is switched off and left hanging from it, and Bacheti et al.\ \cite{tensionbased} reposition the team before a release. None gives a number for the load's attitude through the event. To the author's knowledge no work has shown a UAV attaching to a payload that a team is already carrying in flight, in simulation or on hardware; Quan et al.\ describe a joining robot only in prose, as one already connected to the payload and its neighbours, with no experiment. A joining UAV must localise its attachment point accurately, the load must be reallocated smoothly across the team, and the existing system must stay stable through the joining transient.

Adding UAVs mid-flight would have practical value. A UAV may need to leave the formation because of a low battery, a mechanical failure or a new assignment, which reduces the team's lift and can compromise the mission \cite{farrasFaultTolerantControlMultiQuadcopter2024}. A replacement that joins mid-flight lets the mission continue without landing the whole team. A lighter team could also pass through a narrow space, a task explored in \cite{tudelft, tensionbased}, with UAVs joining afterwards to restore stability and capacity.

A mission could also start with the fewest UAVs and add more only when needed, saving energy. Adding UAVs is thus both a robustness feature and a way to widen the operating envelope of cooperative transport (Figure~\ref{fig:mission_profile}).

This thesis addresses the multi-UAV control problem of keeping the payload stable through a departure and a mid-mission addition, Phases 2, 4 and 6 of Figure~\ref{fig:mission_profile}. The docking mechanism of Phases 4 and 5 is developed by a collaborating student.

The contribution of this thesis is therefore the control around the docking: redistributing the load, re-stabilising the formation and recovering coordinated transport. Validation is in simulation and on a hardware rig.
