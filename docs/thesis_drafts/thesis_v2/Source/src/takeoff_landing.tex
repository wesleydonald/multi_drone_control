\subsection{Takeoff, lift and landing}
\label{sec:takeoff}

The planner's formulation assumes taut rods, but every flight starts and ends with the payload on
the floor and the rods slack or flat. This section describes how the flight gets from the floor into
the regime the planner models, and back.

\paragraph{Why the planner cannot start from the floor.} With the payload resting and the rods
lying flat, the UAVs sit about 0.7\,m from the payload's centre. The planner's references put them
on the 45$^\circ$ cone around the payload, 18--22\,cm further in. Started from rest, the planner
pulled the UAVs inwards over the payload instead of lifting it. This was the cause of the first
failed lifts on the rig.

\paragraph{Rod sweep (creep).} On TAKEOFF each UAV is anchored where it rests. Its rod is swept
about its own attachment point on the payload, at a constant rate, from the elevation it was
measured at to the working elevation of 45$^\circ$. The UAV's reference is the far end of the rod
on that arc, so the rod stays straight and the UAV never moves over the payload. The planner takes
over (the hand-over) once every rod has been at least 37$^\circ$ for 1\,s, or 3\,s after the sweep
ends. The hand-over is refused below 30$^\circ$, because from shallower rods the lift needs two to
three times the tension, mostly sideways. In practice the UAVs lead the arc by 11--15$^\circ$ in the
first second after they leave the floor, so the planner takes over at 51--56$^\circ$ in both the
Gazebo twin and on the rig.

\paragraph{Rod lengths.} At the hand-over the planner measures each rod from motion capture, as
the distance from the UAV's pivot to its attachment point, and uses the measured lengths in its
model and in its tautness gate. A measurement is accepted if each rod is within 25\,\% of the typed
length and the rods agree within 8\,cm; otherwise the typed length is kept and the reason is logged.
Without this, a rod shorter than typed reads as partly slack to the gate
(Section~\ref{sec:sim_robustness}).

\paragraph{Pretension.} The rods are then loaded before the payload leaves the floor. Every UAV's
share of the cable feedforward rises together from zero to its planned value over 3\,s, while the
height target stays at the resting height. A step in the planned pull here launched the payload and
released two magnets on the rig, and UAVs pulling one after another tilted it.

\paragraph{Breakaway.} When the payload rises 1\,cm, or its vertical speed exceeds 0.03\,m/s, it has
broken free of the floor. The fraction of the planned pull reached at that moment is frozen as a
cap on the feedforward. Continuing the ramp to full pull with the payload already in the air ran
it upwards at 0.38\,m/s on the rig. Once the lift starts, the cap is released back to full pull over
2\,s. Holding it for the whole flight, as in the first rig flights, leaves the trackers believing the
rods pull less than they do, and the payload settles low (Section~\ref{sec:rig_results}).

\paragraph{Lift.} The height target then ramps from the resting height to the target height at
0.1\,m/s, easing in over the first 1.2\,s and out over the
last 8\,cm. The trajectory starts when the ramp completes.

\paragraph{Landing.} On LAND the planner lowers the payload's height target at 0.2\,m/s, with the
formation shape unchanged, until the payload rests on the floor. A rigid rod then constrains its UAV:
a UAV descending straight down from its hover point is pushed outward as its rod lengthens
horizontally, by 16--20\,cm in the rig twin, and this tipped UAVs over in a few landings. Three
measures remove this.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unwind.} Each UAV's reference moves out along its rod's circle about the grounded
    attachment point, at the landing speed, down to level, and then straight down. Below 0.25\,m the
    horizontal reference follows the UAV.
    \item \textbf{Feedforward ramp.} The planner blends each UAV's thrust and cable feedforward to plain
    hover over 1\,s. The trackers fly that blend, instead of dropping the cable term in one step at
    touchdown, which left the attitude oscillating while the rods still acted.
    \item \textbf{Ground idle.} A UAV that has flown and is back within 3\,cm of its resting height, with
    the payload down and its reference at the floor, switches to armed idle until the fleet disarms.
    Otherwise a UAV that lands first keeps tracking a reference below the floor and rocks on its feet.
\end{itemize}
Touchdown is detected when the UAVs stop following the descending reference for 1\,s, after they
have descended at least 5\,cm. The planner then reports the fleet landed and the fleet manager disarms
it.
