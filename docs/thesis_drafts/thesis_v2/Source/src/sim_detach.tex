\subsection{Detach}
\label{sec:sim_detach}

This section answers the leave half of RQ1: does a detach by OCP resize keep the ring level when one UAV
of four leaves? The baseline is a hand-off of the payload to the dissipative network on the same
detach, and for the geometry the even layout; the bar is the ring's tilt back under the hover's own
maximum plus 0.5$^\circ$ (Table~\ref{tab:detach}).

\paragraph{The transient.} Figure~\ref{fig:detach} shows one detach in the rig twin: four UAVs on plates 1/3/5/9 in hover, the
UAV on plate~3 released, with $\epsilon = 0$. The uneven 1/3/5/9 layout loads the UAV on plate~9 most.
At DETACH the planner, re-solved for the three survivors on their actual plates, hands the released
UAV's load to them in one 0.1\,s tick. The ring tilts by at most 3.3$^\circ$ and is back within its
hover band after 1\,s.

\paragraph{On the rig.}\label{sec:res_detach_rig} The detach was flown on the rig on 8 October in four flights
(Table~\ref{tab:rig_oct8}): announced, where the planner releases the magnet and resizes at once;
unannounced, where the magnet is switched off without telling the planner, which finds the detach from the
stretched rod; unannounced with a 150$^\circ$ gap left in the ring; and unannounced during the circle. Every
detach landed with the three remaining UAVs still carrying the ring, and in the circle they carried on along
the path without a pause. The planner found the unannounced detaches 0.33--0.42\,s after the magnet was
switched off, which includes the magnet's own release time. Figure~\ref{fig:rig_detach} sets the three hover
detaches against the twin. The unannounced detaches stayed within the twin's spread in tilt and dipped about
1\,cm more. The announced detach rose 11$^\circ$ above its level before the release against the twin's
6--7$^\circ$, and dipped 6.9\,cm against 2.6--3.0\,cm; it is a single run, so whether the rig's announced
release is systematically harsher than the twin's is open.

\paragraph{A constant offset.} All four detach flights held the ring 5--6.5\,cm to one side of its reference,
against 1.9\,cm in the carry flights of the day before. The detach flights ran the planner's reference height
integral, because the model integral was not yet allowed on a fleet that changes size; the carry flights ran the
model integral (Section~\ref{sec:res_rig_integral}). In the circle the offset made the path error 6.8\,cm; with the
circle's mean offset and the lag removed, the remaining error was 2.4\,cm. The model integral is now kept through a
resize: in the twin it reduced the hover offset of the same detach configurations from about 3\,cm to 2\,cm.
\redo{the model integral through a resize on the rig: a hover detach and the circle detach, against the 8 October
offset of 5--6.5\,cm (rig, 14 October).}

\paragraph{The freed UAV's own landing.} After a detach the freed UAV lands by itself, off the ring
(Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}). On the rig on 8 October this landing did not finish: in all four detach
flights the freed UAV floated above its resting height until the fleet's disarm at the end of the
landing dropped it about 0.3\,m (Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}a), and the twin shows the same stall
(Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}b). Its landing was therefore changed: its height reference is pushed
down to its own resting height, and once it is there it is disarmed alone, without waiting for the
fleet (Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}c, in the twin). Figure~\ref{fig:rig_departed} shows the
change on the rig.

\paragraph{Fewer than three.} Detaching from three UAVs to two is not attempted. A rigid payload on two
rim points is a pendulum about the line between them, and its centre of mass hangs off that line.
The planner refuses a detach that would leave fewer than three
UAVs.
