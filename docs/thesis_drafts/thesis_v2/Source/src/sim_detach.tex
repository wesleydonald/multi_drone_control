\subsection{Detach}
\label{sec:sim_detach}

This section answers RQ2: does a detach by OCP resize keep the payload level when one UAV of four
leaves? The baseline is a hand-off of the payload to the dissipative network on the same detach, and for
the geometry the even layout; the bar is the ring's tilt back under the hover's own maximum plus
0.5$^\circ$ (Table~\ref{tab:detach}).

\paragraph{The transient.} Figure~\ref{fig:detach} shows one detach in the rig twin: four UAVs on plates 1/3/5/9
in hover (a layout that loads the UAV on plate~9 most), the UAV on plate~3 released, with $\epsilon = 0$. At
DETACH the planner, re-solved for the three survivors on their actual plates, hands the released UAV's load to
them in one 0.1\,s tick. The ring tilts by at most 3.3$^\circ$ and is back within its hover band after 1\,s.
Over five runs the peak was 2.8--4.0$^\circ$, back in band after 0.9--1.0\,s, with a height change of
3.4--3.6\,cm, against 3.2$^\circ$, 2.6\,s and 2.7\,cm in one earlier rehearsal with the relaxed box and the
height integral on (Table~\ref{tab:detach}, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_tests}): the five runs agree closely.

\paragraph{On the rig.}\label{sec:res_detach_rig} The detach was flown on the rig on 8 October in four flights
(Table~\ref{tab:rig_oct8}): announced, where the planner releases the magnet and resizes at once; unannounced,
where the magnet is switched off without telling the planner, which finds the detach from the stretched rod;
unannounced with a 150$^\circ$ gap left in the ring; and unannounced during the circle. Every detach landed with
the three remaining UAVs still carrying the ring, and in the circle the planner held the path for 1.5\,s
from the detection and then carried the ring on round it. The planner found the unannounced detaches 0.33--0.42\,s after the magnet was switched off, the magnet's
own release time included. Against the twin (Figure~\ref{fig:rig_detach}) the unannounced detaches stayed
within its spread in tilt and dipped about 1\,cm more; the single announced detach rose and dipped more than
the twin, so whether the rig's announced release is systematically harsher is open.

\paragraph{The circle detach.} During the circle (Figure~\ref{fig:rig_circle_detach}) the planner found the
unannounced detach 0.34\,s after the magnet was switched off, held the path for 1.5\,s while the three survivors
took up the freed UAV's share, and then carried the ring on round the circle; the ring tilted 8.1$^\circ$ and dipped
3.9\,cm, against 6.0--7.3$^\circ$ and 2.1--2.5\,cm in the twin, while the freed UAV cleared the circle and began
its own landing 6.1\,s after the release.

\paragraph{A constant offset.} All four detach flights held the ring 5--6.5\,cm to one side of its reference,
against 1.9\,cm in the carry flights of the day before, because they ran the reference-shift height integral (the
model integral was not yet allowed on a fleet that changes size) where the carry flights ran the model integral
(Section~\ref{sec:res_rig_integral}). In the circle the offset made the path error 6.8\,cm; with the mean offset
and the lag removed, 2.4\,cm remained. The model integral is now kept through a resize: in the twin it reduced the
hover offset of the same detach configurations from about 3\,cm to 2\,cm.
\redo{the model integral through a resize on the rig: a hover detach and the circle detach, against the 8 October
offset of 5--6.5\,cm (rig, 14 October).}

\paragraph{The freed UAV's own landing.} On the rig on 8 October the freed UAV's own landing
(Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}) did not finish: in all four detach flights it floated above its resting height
until the fleet's disarm at the end of the landing dropped it about 0.3\,m (Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}a),
and the twin shows the same stall (Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}b). Its height reference is now pushed down
to its own resting height, and once there it is disarmed alone, without waiting for the fleet
(Figure~\ref{fig:departed_landing}c in the twin; Figure~\ref{fig:rig_departed} on the rig).

\paragraph{Who is disarmed when.} Only a UAV off the ring is ever disarmed alone
(Figure~\ref{fig:disarm_lanes}): a newcomer that fails in its transit is disarmed at once while the carriers land
the ring, where the old rule disarmed all four in the air, and a freed carrier is disarmed once it is down at its
rest, about 8\,s after the release, while the others fly on until LAND.

\paragraph{Fewer than three.} Detaching from three UAVs to two is not attempted: a rigid payload on two rim
points is a pendulum about the line between them, with its centre of mass off that line, so the planner refuses
a detach that would leave fewer than three UAVs.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.} Yes, as far as it has been flown: in the twin a detach by OCP resize brought the
ring back within its hover band in 0.9--1.0\,s, and on the rig the three remaining UAVs carried the ring on
through all four detaches, announced and unannounced, in hover and on the circle, with peak tilts of
