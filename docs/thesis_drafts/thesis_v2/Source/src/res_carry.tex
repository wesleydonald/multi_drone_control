\subsection{Carrying with a fixed team}
\label{sec:res_carry}

This section answers RQ1: can the cascade carry the ring with a fixed team, from the floor through hover
and trajectories to the landing, in the rig twin and on the rig? The baseline is the rig twin flying the
same references, and on the rig the same paths flown before the trackers used the rod pull they measure.
The bars are the planner's solve inside its 100\,ms tick, landings in which no UAV tips, and agreement
between the twin and the rig. This carry is in turn the baseline for RQ1a, RQ1b and RQ2.

\subsubsection{Takeoff and lift}

Figure~\ref{fig:flight} shows one whole flight of the rig twin. In the first second after they leave
the floor the UAVs lead the rod sweep's arc by 11--15$^\circ$, so the planner takes over at
51--56$^\circ$ in both the Gazebo twin and on the rig (Section~\ref{sec:takeoff}).

\subsubsection{Trajectories}


Table~\ref{tab:traj} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app_tests}) gives the tracking of each reference kind of
Section~\ref{sec:trajectories} in the rig twin with $\epsilon = 0$, three or four UAVs from a floor start,
with the integral off, so its height column is the static offset that the height integral removes
(Section~\ref{sec:disturbance}). The horizontal error stays about a centimetre on average and under
3\,cm at worst for every reference, with three UAVs or four. The spin turns the ring once about its
vertical axis while it circles, and the ring follows that yaw within a few degrees.

\subsubsection{Rig flights}
\label{sec:rig_results}

The hardware campaign so far covers carrying the ring with three and four UAVs and, since 7 October,
detaching one UAV in flight, announced and unannounced, from a hover and during the circle
(Section~\ref{sec:res_detach_rig}); the attach has not yet been flown on the rig. The rig days, and what
each changed, are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app_rig}, with the six flights of 1 October (three UAVs to
1.0\,m) in Table~\ref{tab:rig_oct1}.
% Table tab:rig_oct1 moved 9 Oct (page trim) to Appendix C.
All six flights lifted cleanly. The rod sweep handed over at 51--55$^\circ$, as in the twin, the
measured rods read 0.531--0.556\,m against 0.55\,m typed, the orbit completed a full lap and the
figure-eight its whole path, and every flight landed. Three results stand out, taken up in
Section~\ref{sec:res_steady}: the ring sat low (Section~\ref{sec:res_cap}), its tilt fell with height
(Section~\ref{sec:res_tilt}), and it drifted towards $-x$ (Section~\ref{sec:res_force}).

\paragraph{7 October: four UAVs and the measured rod pull.} On 7 October four UAVs carried the ring on the
circle and the figure-eight, first with the planned rod pull and then with the trackers' measured pull and the
rods planned at 60$^\circ$ (Section~\ref{sec:tracker}; Table~\ref{tab:rig_oct7}). Both paths flew about three
times closer to their references, and both lags halved. Two things changed at once, so the table shows their
combined effect: the measured pull alone flew the rods at the planned 45$^\circ$, where a magnet let go 3.9\,s
into the circle, and planning 60$^\circ$ keeps each magnet's pull closer to its axis.

\subsubsection{Thrust identification}
\label{sec:thrust_id}

The 13 free hovers of Section~\ref{sec:tracker} fit
\begin{equation}
    u_T = a_i + 0.507\,M - 0.022\,(V - 23.5),
    \label{eq:thrust_fit}
\end{equation}
with $u_T$ the throttle, $M$ the mass carried (kg), $V$ the battery voltage and offsets $a_i$ of
0.181--0.187 for the four UAVs, with an rms residual of 0.003 (Figure~\ref{fig:thrust_map}). Above its
offset the map has a gain of 35.2\,m/s$^2$ per unit throttle for a 0.55\,kg UAV, which is the $k_T$ of
the tracker model \eqref{eq:trk_model}. With the map and one shared offset, the four UAVs hovered free
at +3.7, $-0.5$, $-0.4$ and +0.9\,cm from their targets with no integral action; the one 3.7\,cm high is
the UAV whose own offset is the lowest. A map identified at hover with one proportional gain had left
the UAVs 14--17\,cm low. Carrying the ring, the throttle the UAVs needed (0.55--0.60) matched what the
map predicted for their share of the load (0.569--0.575).

\subsubsection{Landing}
\label{sec:sim_landing}


Over every landing flown in the rig twin with $\epsilon = 0$ (three and four UAVs, hover, the pushes, the
thrust-map errors, the circle and the detach; Figure~\ref{fig:landing} and Table~\ref{tab:land},
Appendix~\ref{sec:app_tests}), a straight descent pushed the UAVs outward and tipped one past 45$^\circ$ in
2 of 21 landings. With the unwind alone the outward push
was gone but 2 of 18 still tipped: one survivor oscillated when the cable term stepped off, and one
departed UAV rocked on the floor. With the feedforward ramp and the ground idle added, none of 18 tipped.

A LAND can come at any moment of a flight, not only from a settled hover. Figure~\ref{fig:land_phases}
presses LAND in each phase: on the floor, in the creep and the lift; in each phase of an attach (the newcomer
parked, climbing, in transit, descending, over the plate, and 0.5, 2 and 4\,s after its weld); after a detach
(the freed UAV stepping out, landing by itself, and after the ring is dropped); twice in a row; and in the
circle. Every UAV landed and was disarmed in 33 of the 34 runs. In the other, LAND 0.5\,s after the weld, the
newcomer, held by the twin's rigid rod, lagged its outward reference while the carriers unwound, pitched over
and stopped the fleet. Over every post-weld LAND flown in the twin this happened
in 3 of 11 runs within 2\,s of a weld and in none of 5 at 4\,s. On the rig the newcomer hangs on a tether that
goes slack, and the rig's landing has no unwind (it lands straight down), so this failure is read as the twin's
rigid link; it is the first thing to watch in the rig's attach flights.

\subsubsection{Computation}
\label{sec:res_compute}

Figure~\ref{fig:solve_time} gives the planner's optimisation time per tick in flight on the rig, 7--8
October, on the laptop that also runs the motion-capture and radio nodes. No tick reached the 100\,ms
budget, but four UAVs leave about 12\,ms of headroom at the 99th percentile, so a fifth UAV would need a
shorter horizon or a faster solver. In Gazebo the same laptop also runs the physics and the
simulated sensors, which slows the solves (Table~\ref{tab:solve_times}, Appendix~\ref{sec:app_tests}), but the
simulation runs at about 0.3 of real time, so the planner still meets its tick in simulated time. The 25 solve
failures of the last rig flight of 30 September were traced to two faults acting together, both now guarded
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app_rig}); none of the 1 October flights had a solve failure.

\paragraph{Answer to RQ1, the carry.} Yes, at lab speeds: on the rig three and then four UAVs carried the ring from the
floor through hover, the circle and the figure-eight, four of them within 3\,cm of the path on average once
the trackers used the rod pull they measure, with no planner tick over its 100\,ms budget; in the twin no UAV
tipped in 18 landings once the unwind, the feedforward ramp and the ground idle were in; and the twin
reproduced the rig's takeoff, solver health and height shortfall but not its horizontal drift or its tilt near
the floor.
