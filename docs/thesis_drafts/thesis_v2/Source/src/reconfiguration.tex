\subsection{Reconfiguration}
\label{sec:reconfig}

A reconfiguration changes the number of UAVs carrying the payload while it is in the air. Both
directions are handled the same way in the final design: the planner switches to a solver for the
new team size and carries on. This subsection describes the detach, the attach, and the design that
was replaced for each.

\subsubsection{Detach}

On DETACH the departing UAV's magnet releases the rod at the payload end, and the UAV flies away with
its rod. The planner continues with the survivors. Two approaches were built and compared.

\paragraph{OCP resize.} The planner keeps flying the payload and simply re-solves for the smaller
fleet. Two properties of the formulation make this possible.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The attachment points $\boldsymbol{\rho}_i$ and rod lengths $l_i$ are runtime parameters of
    the model, not constants compiled into the solver. Only the fleet size fixes the problem
    dimensions, so one solver per fleet size, built in advance, covers any attachment layout.
    \item The survivors keep their own attachment points. The rods stay where they were on the ring,
    so three survivors of a four-UAV ring sit, for example, at 0, 90 and 180$^\circ$, not at an even
    0, 120 and 240$^\circ$. Handing the planner an even ring instead makes its moment balance wrong by
    construction.
    \redo{the hand-back to an even ring after a detach from four UAVs (the survivors at 0, 90 and 180$^\circ$, the planner told 0, 120 and 240$^\circ$): payload tilt.}
% --- original text, flown before the rig twin; kept to refill this place:
%     0, 120 and 240$^\circ$. Handing the planner an even ring instead makes its moment balance wrong by
%     construction; in an early test this tipped the payload by 62$^\circ$.
\end{itemize}
At the detach the planner swaps to the solver for $N-1$ UAVs, sets the survivors' attachment points
and rod lengths, and warm-starts the new problem from the measured state before it flies. The
tensions and cable directions are free at node~0, so the survivors' share of the load is
redistributed by the first solve. No separate hand-off phase is needed. A detach is refused if it
would leave fewer than three UAVs, and during LAND.

\paragraph{Network hand-off.} The alternative, closer to the decentralised literature
\cite{quan2025swarm}, hands the payload to the dissipative network of Section~\ref{sec:dissipative}
on the detach. The network holds the payload while it redistributes the load between the survivors,
then hands back to a planner built for $N-1$. The two are compared in
Section~\ref{sec:sim_detach}. The OCP resize is the detach used in the rest of this work.

\paragraph{The departing UAV.} After the release the departing UAV holds the position where it
detached. On LAND it steps out radially, clear of the ring and the survivors, and descends on its own.
On the rig the release is the UAV's magnet channel, switched by the same DETACH command.

\paragraph{Layout.} Whether the payload can still hang level after a detach depends on the
geometry, not on the controller. The payload's centre of mass must stay inside the polygon of the
surviving attachment points. Removing one UAV from an even ring of $N$ leaves a largest angular gap of
$4\pi/N$, which keeps the centre inside only for $N \ge 5$; $N = 4$ sits exactly on the boundary.
The four-UAV flights therefore use the magnet plates at 30, 90, 150 and 270$^\circ$. Detaching the
UAV at 90$^\circ$ leaves an even triangle at 30, 150 and 270$^\circ$, under which the ring hangs
level. With an uneven layout the tensions are no longer equal: the planner's nominal tensions come
from a moment balance at the actual attachment points, so the payload's reference stays level.

\paragraph{Landing after a detach.} A detach changes which UAVs land with the payload. Touchdown is
judged on the survivors only, so a departed UAV still descending elsewhere does not hold the
survivors' references down. The departed UAVs are judged by their own stall, and the fleet is
declared landed only when they are down too. The cable feedforward is switched off once the payload
rests, because its sideways component pushes a grounded survivor across the floor.

\subsubsection{Attach}

On ATTACH a free UAV joins the carried payload: it approaches its attachment point, its magnet welds
to a plate on the ring, and the planner folds it into the team. The approach controller, the
approach UAV and its magnet are the collaborating student's, and this work takes over at the weld.
% TODO: cite the collaborator's thesis or report once available.

\paragraph{Sequence.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target hold.} ATTACH freezes the payload's horizontal reference, or (when the
    payload is moving) keeps it on its trajectory. In both cases the reference is shifted onto the
    measured payload position, so the target the newcomer approaches is where the payload actually is.
    \item \textbf{Approach.} The newcomer is flown to a point above its plate and descends onto it.
    Either the collaborator's approach controller flies it, or the newcomer's own tracker follows an
    approach profile from this work. The payload may keep moving, at up to 0.2\,m/s.
    \redo{a weld onto a payload moving at 0.4\,m/s against 0.2\,m/s (the limit on the payload's speed during the approach).}
% --- original text, flown before the rig twin; kept to refill this place:
%     approach profile from this work. The payload may keep moving, at up to 0.2\,m/s; at 0.4\,m/s the
%     weld flipped the payload in simulation.
    \item \textbf{Weld.} A magnet manager welds when the magnet tip is within 8\,cm of the plate
    point, the relative speed is under 0.05\,m/s, and both have held for 0.15\,s. The plate point
    is computed from the payload's measured pose, so it moves and turns with the ring.
    \item \textbf{Authority hand-over.} Until the weld, a multiplexer forwards the approach
    controller's commands to the newcomer's radio. After the weld it switches to this work's
    tracker, but only on a command that is armed and above idle throttle.
    The switch is latched. An abort latches the mux to a disarmed idle command until it is restarted.
    \redo{the authority hand-over at the weld with the mux switching on an armed but idle stream, against the armed-and-above-idle rule: whether the newcomer's motors cut at the weld.}
% --- original text, flown before the rig twin; kept to refill this place:
%     tracker, but only on a command that is armed and above idle throttle. A mux that handed authority
%     to an armed but idle stream cut the newcomer's motors at every weld in early tests. The switch is
%     latched. An abort latches the mux to a disarmed idle command until it is restarted.
    \item \textbf{Fold-in.} The planner records the weld point in the payload frame and switches to
    the solver for $N+1$ UAVs, with the newcomer's tension starting at 0.1\,N and every UAV's tension
    blended to the balanced solution for the new geometry.
    \redo{the fold-in with the newcomer's tension starting at 1\,N against 0.1\,N: payload tilt at the weld.}
% --- original text, flown before the rig twin; kept to refill this place:
%     blended to the balanced solution for the new geometry. A newcomer started at 1\,N of tension
%     pulled the payload with a lever about the incumbents' cable apex and tilted it at the weld.
    \item \textbf{Resume.} The trajectory continues with $N+1$ UAVs.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Replaced design: network attach.} Until mid-September the attach handed the payload to
the dissipative network at the weld. The newcomer was welded as a central lifter and handed out to
its ring slot over 8--12\,s, with the incumbents' tensions blended over the same ramp. The two
designs are compared in Section~\ref{sec:sim_attach}.

\paragraph{The rejoin mission.} The full mission of Figure~\ref{fig:mission_profile} is assembled with
the collaborator's mission planner. Four UAVs carry the ring around the orbit. One detaches by OCP
resize and is released to the collaborator's mission, which picks up an object with its magnet and
drops it into the ring. It is then handed back at its attach-ready point, approaches, welds and is
folded back in by OCP resize, after which the fleet lands. A ground-start variant has the
collaborator's controller join four UAVs to the ring on the floor and hand them over to this work's
planner, which lifts and carries it.
