\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{sec:rq}

The research question of this thesis is whether a centralised controller can manage the join and
leave transients of individual UAVs in a multi-UAV payload system while keeping the payload
stable. The payload is a 0.86\,kg ring carried on rigid rods (Section~\ref{sec:hardware}). The
controller is a cascade: a centralised planner solves an optimal control problem (OCP) for the
payload and every rod, and a tracking controller per UAV follows its share of the plan. A UAV leaving
or joining resizes the planner's OCP to the new team (the OCP resize, Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}); a
joining UAV is called the newcomer. Two questions establish the carry with a fixed team, and three
test the reconfiguration.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=3.6em, labelwidth=3.0em, labelsep=0.6em, align=left]
    \item[\textbf{Q1}] \textbf{Carry.} Can the cascade carry the ring with a fixed team, from the floor,
    on the rig?
    \item[\textbf{Q2}] \textbf{Steady accuracy.} What leaves a steady payload error, and what removes it?
    \item[\textbf{RQ1a}] \textbf{Leave.} Does a detach by OCP resize keep the payload level?
    \item[\textbf{RQ1b}] \textbf{Join.} Does an attach by OCP resize fold a newcomer in with a small
    transient?
    \item[\textbf{RQ2}] \textbf{Trajectory-based against dissipative.} Does a trajectory-based optimal
    control approach \cite{tudelft} or a decentralised dissipative control law \cite{quan2025swarm}
    better balance robustness and tracking, on the same plant and trackers?
\end{itemize}
Q1 and Q2 are the baseline for the other three: a reconfiguration can only be judged against a team
that carries the payload well before anything changes. The working hypothesis is that the centralised
approach suits a change of team composition, because the coordinating node can recompute the force
distribution for the new team at the moment of the event, rather than relying on the remaining UAVs
to absorb it passively.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

This thesis contributes the following, in the order of the questions they answer.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A centralised payload controller flown on commercial-grade hardware.} The
    load--cable planner of \cite{tudelft} is combined with a cable-aware tracking model predictive
    controller (MPC) per UAV that commands Betaflight flight controllers over their radio links,
    with no changes to the UAVs' firmware, a floor start for rigid rods, a pretension and lift
    procedure, and a thrust map identified on the rig (Sections~\ref{sec:controller}, \ref{sec:tracker}
    and~\ref{sec:takeoff}). On the rig, three and then four UAVs carried the 0.86\,kg ring at up to
    1.0\,m, around a circle and through a figure-eight (Section~\ref{sec:res_carry}; Q1).
    \item \textbf{An analysis of steady errors in the cascade.} Four sources of steady payload error
    were separated (a relaxed initial condition in the tracker, a breakaway pull cap held for the whole
    flight, model errors in thrust and rod mass, and external forces), and integral action in the
    planner's model and estimators from the literature were compared against them
    (Section~\ref{sec:res_steady}; Q2).
    \item \textbf{Reconfiguration by resizing the optimal control problem.} The attachment
    geometry is a runtime parameter of the planner, so a detach or an attach becomes a switch to
    a pre-built solver for the new team size, with each UAV kept on its real attachment point
    (Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}). On the rig the three remaining UAVs carried the ring on through four
    detaches, announced and unannounced, in hover and on the circle (Section~\ref{sec:sim_detach}; RQ1a);
    the attach is in Section~\ref{sec:sim_attach} (RQ1b).
    \redo{OCP resize against handing the payload to a dissipative network during the transient, on a detach and on an attach.}

    \item \textbf{A geometric condition for a level detach.} Whether the payload can stay level
    after a UAV leaves depends on whether its centre of mass stays inside the polygon of the
    surviving attachment points. This sets the attachment layout for four UAVs
    (Sections~\ref{sec:reconfig} and~\ref{sec:sim_detach}; RQ1a).
    \item \textbf{Mid-flight attachment of a new UAV.} A free UAV welds onto the carried payload
    through an electromagnet and is folded into the planner, in combination with a collaborator's
    approach controller (Section~\ref{sec:sim_attach}; RQ1b).

\subsection{Outline}

Section~\ref{sec:lit} reviews the literature and states the gap. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the
rig, its model, the controller, the floor start and landing, the reconfiguration, the dissipative
alternative and the experimental method. Section~\ref{sec:results} answers the questions in the order of
Table~\ref{tab:rq_map} and discusses them against the literature, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes. The appendices hold the software, the parameters, the rig operations, the negative results
and further tests, and the timeline.
