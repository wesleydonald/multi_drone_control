## Topic 1: plug-and-play / variable-dimension MPC

### Papers

1. **Riverso, S., Farina, M., Ferrari-Trecate, G.** (2013). Plug-and-play decentralized model predictive
   control for linear systems. *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 58(10), 2608-2614.
   DOI 10.1109/TAC.2013.2254641. **Peer-reviewed** (technical note). Full text read (arXiv 1302.0226,
   the technical report the note was drawn from).
   *Shows:* a linear system of coupled subsystems is controlled by local tube MPCs. When a subsystem
   is plugged in, only the controllers of the subsystems it couples to are redesigned, and an unplug
   needs no redesign. A change the redesign cannot certify is refused. Both operations are offline, and
   the example is frequency control in a power network.
   *Fit:* the founding plug-and-play MPC result, which the task names.

2. **Zeilinger, M. N., Pu, Y., Riverso, S., Ferrari-Trecate, G., Jones, C. N.** (2013). Plug and play
   distributed model predictive control based on distributed invariance and optimization. *52nd IEEE
   Conference on Decision and Control (CDC)*, 5770-5776. DOI 10.1109/CDC.2013.6760799.
   **Peer-reviewed** (conference). Abstract read.
   *Shows:* plug-and-play operation for strongly coupled linear subsystems under iterative distributed
   MPC. The local control laws are re-synthesised in a distributed way, and a transition scheme prepares
   the system before the change. A new topology that would be infeasible is rejected. Power-network
   example.
   *Fit:* the distributed counterpart named in the task, and the source of the "transition phase" idea.

3. **Aboudonia, A., Martinelli, A., Hoischen, N., Lygeros, J.** (2022). Reconfigurable plug-and-play
   distributed model predictive control for reference tracking. *2022 IEEE 61st CDC*, 1130-1135.
   DOI 10.1109/CDC51059.2022.9993380. **Peer-reviewed** (conference). Full text read (arXiv
   2207.09233v2).
   *Shows:* a redesign phase (passivity-based) keeps the network stable. A transition phase then steers
   the old network and the incoming subsystems to steady states from which the new MPC is feasible; the
   request is accepted only if that transition problem is feasible. In a simulated network of eight
   mass-spring-damper systems, each transition took 4 sampling intervals, and 100 of 100 requests were
   feasible against 15 of 100 for the benchmark (Sec. 4, Table 1; arXiv numbers).
   *Fit:* the most recent plug-and-play MPC with the transition made explicit.

4. **Saccani, D., Fagiano, L., Zeilinger, M. N., Carron, A.** (2023). Model predictive control for
   multi-agent systems under limited communication and time-varying network topology. *2023 62nd IEEE
   CDC*, 3764-3769. DOI 10.1109/CDC49753.2023.10383790. **Peer-reviewed** (conference). Full text
   read (arXiv 2304.01649v1).
   *Shows:* each robot (nonlinear discrete-time dynamics) predicts two trajectories in one problem: one
   towards its target assuming the topology does not change, and one safe trajectory that keeps the
   constraints if new agents join within the horizon. Plug-in and plug-out therefore need no request.
   The paper proves recursive feasibility and stability. It is shown in simulation only, with eight
   ground vehicles at an intersection.
   *Fit:* the closest MPC work to a robot team whose membership changes, and motivated by robot swarms.

### Synthesis (replaces the CHECK at `Source/src/control.tex:154-156`, first of three paragraphs)

```latex
Plug-and-play model predictive control lets subsystems join or leave a running network without losing
stability or constraint satisfaction, provided each change is prepared before it happens. Riverso et al.\
\cite{riverso2013plug} redesign offline only the controllers of the subsystems a newcomer couples to and
refuse a change the redesign cannot certify, Zeilinger et al.\ \cite{zeilinger2013plug} and Aboudonia et
al.\ \cite{aboudonia2022reconfigurable} add a transition phase that prepares the network before the switch,
and Saccani et al.\ \cite{saccani2023model} remove the request by having each robot also plan a safe
trajectory that holds if others join within the horizon. All four are shown in simulation, on linear
coupled subsystems such as power networks and coupled masses or on ground robots coupled only through
collision constraints. A suspended payload couples every UAV through the load, and a weld or an unannounced
loss happens at an instant the planner does not choose; this thesis instead switches a centralised
nonlinear OCP to a pre-built problem of the new size at the event, warm-started from the measured state,
so the first solve after the switch is shown in flight rather than guaranteed.
```

## Topic 2: static equilibrium of multi-robot suspended loads

### Papers

1. **Michael, N., Fink, J., Kumar, V.** (2011). Cooperative manipulation and transportation with aerial
   robots. *Autonomous Robots* 30(1), 73-86. DOI 10.1007/s10514-010-9205-0. **Peer-reviewed**
   (journal). Abstract read, plus the full text of the RSS 2009 conference version (same authors, title
   and abstract). The journal PDF is paywalled and was not read.
   *Shows:* the static equilibrium of a payload hung from n robots by cables, and, for three robots,
   closed-form robot positions and tensions that respect tension bounds.
   - Three robots are the fewest that hold a payload in equilibrium over a large set of poses, and the
     authors require the three attachment points to be non-collinear and to span the centre of mass.
   - With hovering robots and taut cables, the payload behaves as if attached through rigid rods and ball
     joints.
   - The robot positions admit several payload equilibria. In their example there are five, of which one
     is stable.
   - It is validated in experiments with three quadrotors.
   *Fit:* the classic result behind "geometry decides". Their span condition is this thesis's polygon
   condition, and their rigid-rod-and-ball-joint view matches the rig's rods.

2. **Jiang, Q., Kumar, V.** (2013). The inverse kinematics of cooperative transport with multiple aerial
   robots. *IEEE Transactions on Robotics* 29(1), 136-145. DOI 10.1109/TRO.2012.2218991.
   **Peer-reviewed** (journal). Abstract read.
   *Shows:* the robot positions that give a payload pose have, in general, no solution for one or two
   robots and infinitely many for three or more. For three robots with the tensions specified there are
   finitely many, all of which an analytic algorithm finds. The paper also gives the set of allowable
   tensions and a stability analysis of every equilibrium.
   *Fit:* the counting result the task names, and why three carriers is the minimum after a detach.

3. **Gabellieri, C., Tognon, M., Sanalitro, D., Franchi, A.** (2023). Equilibria, stability, and
   sensitivity for the aerial suspended beam robotic system subject to parameter uncertainty. *IEEE
   Transactions on Robotics* 39(5), 3977-3993. DOI 10.1109/TRO.2023.3279033. **Peer-reviewed**
   (journal). Full text read (arXiv 2302.07031v3).
   *Shows:* for two UAVs carrying a beam on cables under a communication-free admittance controller, the
   internal force from non-vertical cables makes the load's attitude equilibrium asymptotically stable
   and its error less sensitive to parameter errors (mass, centre-of-mass offset, length). With zero
   internal force (vertical cables), any mismatch drives the load towards vertical. In hardware the pitch
   error was about 18° with zero internal force against about 2.5° with a positive one (Sec. VII-A,
   Fig. 14).
   *Fit:* recent, with hardware. It explains why non-vertical rods (the rig's 45-60° elevation) help hold
   the load level. It is a different paper from `gabellieri2023force` (IROS), which is already cited.

4. **Liu, S., Caro, S., Chriette, A., Fantoni, I.** (2025). Wrench capability analysis of a cooperative
   multi-unmanned aerial vehicle parallel robot with rigid links. *Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics*
   17(8), 081011. DOI 10.1115/1.4068272. **Peer-reviewed** (journal). Abstract read; no open PDF found.
   *Shows:* for UAVs holding a platform through rigid links, the quasi-static equilibrium gives the
   platform's available wrench set from each quadrotor's available thrust set. A capacity margin then
   compares configurations. It is validated in ROS simulation and on a real robot.
   *Fit:* recent, and the closest model to the rig's rigid rods. It turns feasibility into a margin for a
   given layout.

### Synthesis (replaces the same CHECK, second paragraph)

```latex
Static equilibrium decides which layouts can hold a suspended payload at all. Michael et al.\
\cite{michael2011cooperative} show that three robots are the fewest that hold a payload over a wide set of
poses, require the attachment points to span its centre of mass, and treat taut cables under hovering
robots as rigid rods on ball joints, which admit several equilibria of which only some are stable; Jiang
and Kumar \cite{jiang2013inverse} show that the robot positions for a given pose are not unique for three
or more robots and become finitely many for three once the tensions are fixed. Gabellieri et al.\
\cite{gabellieri2023equilibria} add that the internal force of non-vertical cables makes the load's
attitude equilibrium stable and less sensitive to model errors, for two robots and a beam in hardware, and
Liu et al.\ \cite{liu2025wrench} compute from the same quasi-static balance how much wrench a platform on
rigid links can still receive within the UAVs' thrust limits. All four analyse a fixed team: none asks what
the equilibrium becomes when one carrier leaves the layout in flight, where this thesis finds that an even
ring of four leaves the centre of mass on the edge of the surviving triangle (Section~\ref{sec:reconfig}).
```

## Topic 3: aerial docking and magnetic attach in flight

### Papers

1. **Saldaña, D., Gabrich, B., Li, G., Yim, M., Kumar, V.** (2018). ModQuad: The flying modular structure
   that self-assembles in midair. *2018 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA)*,
   691-698. DOI 10.1109/ICRA.2018.8461014. **Peer-reviewed** (conference). Full text read (author copy,
   lehigh.edu).
   *Shows:*
   - The modules are quadrotors in cuboid frames with passive NdFeB magnets: about a 1 kg bond for a
     40 g module, and the magnet's pull corrects misalignments under 25 mm.
   - One structure hovers while the other approaches along a "tunnel" at a constant 0.1 m/s.
   - A node that watches motion capture detects the new structure and uploads a new force distribution
     to every module, after which the structure recovers its stability.
   - Undocking is left to future work.
   *Fit:* the founding mid-air docking paper, which the task names. Its magnetic dock and its controller
   update after detection parallel this thesis's weld and fold-in.

2. **Sugihara, J., Nishio, T., Nagato, K., Nakao, M., Zhao, M.** (2023). Design, control, and motion
   strategy of TRADY: Tilted-rotor-equipped aerial robot with autonomous in-flight assembly and
   disassembly ability. *Advanced Intelligent Systems* 5(10), 2300191. DOI 10.1002/aisy.202300191.
   **Peer-reviewed** (journal, open access). Full text read (arXiv 2303.07106v3).
   *Shows:*
   - Two units dock in flight through a switchable permanent magnet, a drogue and servo-driven pegs, and
     switch from under-actuated to fully actuated control.
   - A thrust blend from the old model to the new one keeps the total thrust steady at the switch. The
     altitude rise after assembly was about 0.065 m with the blend against about 0.39 m, with unstable
     control, without it (Sec. VI-C2, Figs. 16-17).
   - Assembly succeeded in 13 of 15 trials and disassembly in 15 of 15 (Sec. VI-C1).
   *Fit:* a recent magnetic dock, both ways, whose finding is that the controller switch at contact must
   be blended. It is the same idea as the fold-in's tension blend from 0.1 N.

3. **Shankar, A., Elbaum, S., Detweiler, C.** (2020). Towards in-flight transfer of payloads between
   multirotors. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 5(4), 6201-6208. DOI 10.1109/LRA.2020.3011393.
   **Peer-reviewed** (journal). Full text read (author copy, unl.edu).
   *Shows:*
   - One multirotor grasps a payload bar held aloft by another on a rigid link about 1 m long, guided by
     monocular vision on a constant-velocity approach.
   - A magnetic feedback element confirms the grasp before the first multirotor is commanded to release.
     The gains are then rescheduled for the new mass.
   - Indoors, 16 of 16 grasps succeeded with the carrier on a semi-rigid mount.
   - Outdoors, with both vehicles airborne, one trial of eight needed a pilot. In the last three the
     worn gripper lost the bar after the release (Sec. V-C, V-D).
   *Fit:* the closest work to joining a load that is already carried. Its confirm-then-release sequence
   mirrors the weld and the authority hand-over.

4. **Zhang, X., Lu, H., Feng, Z., Xiao, X.** (2026). Design and implementation of EPM based modular
   micro-UAVs for autonomous midair docking. *IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters* 11(1), 514-521.
   DOI 10.1109/LRA.2025.3632750. **Peer-reviewed** (journal). Abstract read; no open PDF found.
   *Shows:* Crazyflie-based 75 g modules dock through an electro-permanent magnet that holds about 1.5 N
   with no static power and switches on a current pulse. A two-phase controller handles the binary
   switch for smooth docking, and docking is shown in flight.
   *Fit:* the most recent magnetic attach mechanism, which switches like the rig's magnet does.

### Synthesis (replaces the same CHECK, third paragraph)

```latex
UAVs dock to one another in flight, often through magnets, and the controller switch at contact matters as
much as the mechanism. ModQuad \cite{saldana2018modquad} docks modules with passive permanent magnets that
pull in small misalignments while the other structure hovers, then uploads a new force distribution to every
module once motion capture detects the new structure, and TRADY \cite{sugihara2023trady}, which assembles and
disassembles through a switchable magnet and pegs, cut the altitude jump after assembly from about 0.39 to
0.065\,m by blending the thrust from the old controller to the new one. Zhang et al.\ \cite{zhang2026epm}
dock micro-UAVs with an electro-permanent magnet that holds without power and switches on a current pulse,
and Shankar et al.\ \cite{shankar2020inflight} come closest to joining a carried payload: one multirotor
grasps a bar held aloft by another, confirms the grasp with a magnetic sensor before the first releases, and
reschedules its gains for the new mass. Each docks to a rigid structure or to a single carrier; none joins
a payload hung from a team, where the newcomer's pull at the weld can tilt the payload and the team's
tensions must be redistributed.
```
