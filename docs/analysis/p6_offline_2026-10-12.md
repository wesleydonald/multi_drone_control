## 2. The slip detector over the 8 Oct bags

**Question.** C2-A: "every ring loss began with a magnet letting go"; did any magnet let go on 8 Oct without a
command, and did the detectors fire correctly?

**Method.** Two detectors exist, and both were replayed over all 13 bags of 8 Oct. The slip detector
(`drop_trigger_replay.py`) checks every rod's pivot-to-plate distance at the full mocap rate (a release is over 3 cm
for 6 samples). The stretch detector that flies (`_detect_tick`) was replayed at the planner's own ticks.

| flight | release | slip detector: OFF to separation onset / to 3 cm (ms) | stretch detector: replay fires (ms after OFF) | flight logged (ms) | over at fire (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r302_3 | planner OFF (announced) | 242 / 255 | no fire (announced; detector off in flight) | - | - |
| r303_4 | operator OFF | 191 / 258 | 410 | 418 | 14.1 |
| r304_3 | operator OFF | 97 / 163 | 332 | 332 | 20.5 |
| r305 | operator OFF | 120 / 199 | 337 | 345 | 15.3 |

Four of the 13 bags flew the ring. Uncommanded releases: none. False alarms: none from either detector. Misses: none.
The survivors' rods never left their length by more than 0.7 cm.

**What it means.** On 8 Oct no magnet let go without a command, so every uncommanded release in the bagged record is
on 7 Oct, all with the rods planned at 45°. The detector that flies caught every unannounced release in 0.33-0.42 s.
That delay is the magnet opening (0.10-0.19 s after the OFF), the rod passing 6 cm before the next tick, and the
two-tick rule (exactly one more tick, 0.1 s).

**Caveats.** The record holds four uncommanded releases in two flights, not three: the first capsize's second
survivor let go at +0.397 s, at about 46° of tilt, inside the capsize. The 7 Oct check (`2_slip/check_2026-10-07/`)
reproduces the 8 Oct replay card's releases. On the 7 Oct measured-pull circle the stretch detector would have fired
0.28 s after the first slip, with the same 17.5 cm the 7 Oct card gives. The card's 0.38 s was timed to the log row's
stamp, which comes about 0.1 s after the tick that read the poses.

## 3. The double-count test at the resize tick

**Question.** Freeze §1.5's double-count variant (T9, T10): on the measured pull, does the resize tick add the load
change a second time? Does the measured term already hold the change the plan adds?

**Method.** Per survivor, from the planner and tracker logs: the plan's vertical tension step, and the measured
vertical pull the tracker applied at the resize, both from the 2 s before the release. Then the commanded thrust
across the resized plan's arrival. The asked pair, two more rig releases and the 7 Oct measured-pull twin arms.

| set | survivors | share of the plan's step already in the measured term at the resize | thrust change at the plan's arrival, as a share of the plan's step |
|---|---|---|---|
| rig r303_4 (unannounced) | 3 | 0.98, 1.04, 0.73 | -0.32, -0.62, -2.95 (against the step) |
| rig r302_3 (announced) | 3 | -0.09, 0.11, 0.15 | 0.19, -0.45, -0.82 |
| rig r303_4, r304_3, r305 (unannounced) | 9 | 0.60-1.52, median 0.93 | against the step's sign in 9 of 9 (0.14-1.40 N) |
| twin R1140, R1145, R1139, R1141 (unannounced) | 12 | 0.55-1.05, median 0.69 | -0.41 to +0.26 (≤ 0.39 N) |
| twin R1118, R1121 (announced) | 6 | -0.02 to 0.01 | 0.59-0.76, with the step, 6 of 6 |

**What it means.**
- Does the measured term already hold the change? On an unannounced release, mostly yes. On an announced release, no:
  the plan steps with the OFF, before the magnet opens.
- Is it counted a second time? Not at the tick: no survivor's commanded thrust took the plan's step again when the
  resized plan arrived.
- What the logs show instead:
  - an announced step reaches the thrust before the load does (in the twin and on the rig);
  - on the rig only, a counter-swing of about 1 N and about 1 Hz starts at the plan's arrival.
- On both unannounced hover releases the tilt rise peaked 0.05-0.15 s before the resized plan reached the trackers, so
  the resize cannot have set their Δθ.
- For the thesis: the double-count variant's frozen signs stand, but its mechanism is not in these logs. The text
  should not explain the "when the resize comes" order by a double count.

**Caveats.** One rig flight per kind (two more as context). The comparison uses the vertical parts, because the rig's
~0.55 N inward force mixes in the horizontal. The rig counter-swing is not explained. One candidate (not tested): the
resized plan lowers the reference of the survivor whose pull falls by 4-5.6 cm in 0.35 s, and the measured term reads
that motion back as rod pull.

## 4. Rig latency: commanded against measured body rates

**Question.** How long from a tracker's command to the drone's body rate on the rig (the "loop latency" the twin does
not model)?

**Method.** The ELRS sticks through the tracker's Betaflight rate curve, against the body rate differentiated from the
mocap quaternions. Both on the bag's receive clock, 18 flights of 7-8 Oct: a cross-correlation peak and a
first-order-plus-delay fit per drone and axis.

| axis | drone-flights | cross-correlation lag (ms), median (IQR) | fitted delay (ms) | fitted lag τ (ms) | gain | r² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| roll | 69 | 24 (20-24) | 20 (20-20) | 2 (floor) | 0.98 | 0.52 |
| pitch | 69 | 24 (24-24) | 20 (20-25) | 2 (2-5) | 1.06 | 0.56 |
| yaw | 36 usable | 102 | 55 | 55 | 1.72 | 0.24 (not usable) |

**What it means.**
- Command to body rate takes about 20-25 ms on the rig (one 50 Hz tracker tick), with no measurable lag after it and a
  gain of 1.
- The tracker's model has no delay and a 0.12 s rate lag, so it treats the rate loop as about five times slower than
  it is.
- This inner loop accounts for 20-25 ms of the 90-160 ms by which the rig's path lag exceeds the twin's (C1). A
  larger candidate, from item 2: the planner reads its poses at a tick's start and publishes 73-87 ms later.

**Caveats.** The loop is closed, so the fit is read for the delay only. Motive's own latency is inside the number.
Yaw is barely excited.

### 5a. The rig Δθ null and the minimum detectable effect at n = 3
**Method.** T6 Δθ at pseudo-events (every 0.5 s) in still rig flight with nothing happening. Then the frozen
resolution rule on 20000 simulated pairs, with noise drawn from the null.

| hover, 7 s | 23 (4; mostly 2) | 4.79 | 0.77 | 6.00 | 4.6 / 4.4 / 9.3 | 3.7 / 3.7 / 7.6 |
| path, 7 s | 107 (5) | 5.58 | 1.21 | 7.22 | 7.2 / 6.9 / 9.3 | 5.8 / 5.9 / 7.6 |
| path, 15 s | 43 (3) | 6.27 | 1.52 | 8.32 | 9.0 / 8.4 / 9.3 | 7.3 / 7.4 / 7.6 |

| flight | Δθ | share of the null at or above | Δθ / σ_pre (null median, p95) | share of the null ratio at or above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| r0012 | 6.07 | 0.04 | 3.11 (3.25, 4.06) | 0.57 |
| r302_3 | 11.21 | 0 | 8.06 | 0 |
| r303_4 | 5.29 | 0.26 | 4.48 | 0 |
| r304_3 | 16.88 | 0 | 16.39 | 0 |
| r305 | 8.02 | 0.14 | 3.44 (3.07, 3.87) | 0.33 |

**What it means.**
- Δθ has a floor on the rig. With nothing happening it reads 5-6°, about three times the ring's own rocking.
- The announced hover release and the 150° release are far above that floor.
- The unannounced 120° hover release is inside the raw null, but above it once scaled by its own, smaller rocking.
- The circle release and the first rig detach are not separable from the null, raw or scaled.
- At n = 3 a rig lever pair resolves about 4.5° in hover and 7-9° on a path. The ramp's 0.8° resolves in 1-2 % of
  pairs.
- Under the frozen rule the MDE grows from n = 2 to n = 3. The bar is twice the larger range, and the expected range
  of three runs is 1.5 times that of two, while the means sharpen only by 1.2.

**Caveats.** The hover null rests mostly on two 7 Oct carry flights, one of them with 100 g extra; no 8 Oct hover held
still long enough. The noise model is a lower bound on run-to-run spread.

## Problems found

**In the rig data and the record:**
1. The record holds **four** uncommanded releases in two flights, not three. The first capsize lost a second survivor
   at +0.397 s, at about 46° of tilt. If counting it out is deliberate, say so.
2. The first rig detach's registry "peak 4.6°" is the peak of its first second (4.58° on the bag). The freeze's
   absolute peak runs to LAND and reads 5.48°, set 0.1 s before LAND, not by the detach; its Δθ is 6.07° at +0.69 s.
   Quote the T6 numbers with their windows.
3. The 11 Oct release table's capsize row (91.3°) ran its window 23.7 s past the fleet abort, onto the ring on the
   floor (fixed: 68.3°).
4. The plan's "3.7-6.9 cm" 8 Oct dip range is the below-reference reading. On the kept scorer it is 3.1-6.9 cm.
5. The 7 Oct unannounced card's "detected 0.38 s after" the measured-pull circle's slip is timed to a log stamp. At
   the planner tick it is 0.28 s.
6. On the rig, the resized plan's arrival starts a 1 Hz counter-swing of about 1 N in the survivors. The twin does not
   do this.
7. The tracker models the rate loop with a 0.12 s lag and no delay. The rig shows a 20 ms delay and no lag.

**In the frozen definitions (freeze §1, signed 11 Oct; nothing changed here):**
1. **Time back in band** (Q2) needs LAND ≥ event + 10 s. Only 1 of the 8 rig release rows can be scored, so T8's
   column will read "not scored" almost everywhere until W4/W5's 15 s holds.
2. **No event time for an uncommanded release, and no window end without LAND.** Three of the eight T8 rows (both
   capsizes and the recovered slip) need a labelled substitute.
3. **Δθ has a no-event floor of about 3 σ_pre** (5-6° on the rig). The freeze reads Δθ against zero, and σ_rig (the
   pooled σ_pre, 1.57°) is not the null's spread (0.8-1.5°). A rig Δθ near 5° cannot be told from no event.
4. **The resolution rule's range bar makes a pair harder to resolve with more runs** (MDE 3.7° at n = 2 against 4.6°
   at n = 3 on the hover null). This matters for "repeats to n ≥ 3".
