# X4 weld kick: root cause (31 Oct 2026)

Read-only analysis for the lab chat. Nothing was flown. Runs R3405, R3407, R3409 (all three 30 min relays) and seven
others with a rejoin and a recorded `gz_record/state.tlog`. Scripts, tables and the two proposed configs:
`results/analysis/x4_kick_2026-10-31/`. Line numbers are the working tree at 31 Oct 20:30 (it was being edited).

Each claim is marked **Logs** (the run's own files), **Binary** (the installed plugins' symbol and call lists, since no
Gazebo sources are installed) or **Inferred**.

## Verdict

The kick is a simulation artefact and it is understood to within 1 %. It is not hinge drift. The stub, a 1 g hinge
link left on the plate after a release, keeps spinning freely at the rate its rod had at that instant. The re-weld
does not exchange momentum: Gazebo's weld makes the rod and the drone take the stub's velocity. The rate at release is
large only on rods welded before, because the first re-weld leaves the rod's end 1.25 cm off the stub's centre, which
turns the rod into a 3.3 Hz undamped pendulum about its own axis. A real magnet has no such link, so nothing of the
3.3 m/s carries to real flight. A re-fly on this world is expected to fail again (section 5).

| Check, on 48 fold-ins in 10 runs (**Logs**) | Result |
|---|---|
| Velocity step measured against stub spin x lever, correlation | 0.999999 |
| Same, median error on the 39 steps over 2 cm/s (largest 13 %, on the smallest steps) | 0.9 % |
| Same, direction, smallest cosine on those 39 | 0.987 |
| Weld with no spin (drone 0's first visit, 4 runs): step | 0.003-0.005 m/s |

## The law, with the faults

Step at weld = stub spin x stub-to-drone lever. The spin vector lies along the rod as it was at release, 30 deg off
vertical (cable elevation 60 deg). At the weld the rod hangs vertical. So **step = spin x 1.04 m x sin 30 = 0.52 x
spin**, tangent to the ring at the plate (median cosine with the tangent 0.9997, 25 fold-ins over 4 cm/s).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R3405 #11 | /drone_1 | 6.193 | 3.100 | 3.113 | 3.337 |
| R3407 #11 | /drone_1 | 7.853 | 3.926 | 3.941 | 4.110 |
| R3409 #10 | /drone_0 | 7.127 | 3.566 | 3.581 | 3.717 |
| R3407 #10 (survived) | /drone_0 | 2.382 | 1.191 | 1.196 | 1.415 |

Peak speed read 1.04-1.19 x the predicted step (fold-ins over 0.2 m/s), so the 3 m/s envelope fires at a spin of about
4.8-5.5 rad/s.

## 1. What happens at a weld

### The plant

File `simulation_assets/eight_rigid_ground_rig_R50_L100.sdf`, generated by `generate_rigid_world.py` (`gen` below).

| Part | SDF lines | gen lines | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|

| Plate hinge | 216-221 | 174-179 | ball joint, ring to stub, `<damping>0.1` in the file |
| Rod | 222-251 | 180-184 | 75 g with its magnet, Izz 3.2e-7, Ixx 8e-3, COM 0.233 m below its centre |
| Top hinge | 252-258 | 185-190 | ball joint, rod to drone, 0.04 m below the body |
| Weld | 619-624 | 239-247 | DetachableJoint, parent the stub, child the rod |

Installed: gz-sim 8.10.0, gz-physics 7.6.0, DART 6.13.2.

### The steps (`dissipative_node.py` unless named)

1. **Plate point.** At a leave the planner keeps the plate point and rod length from its own arrays (:1697-1698,
   `_stage_departed` :3430-3440). The rejoin aims the rod's end at that point on the measured ring (`_plate_world`
   :3509).
2. **The gate.** `rejoin_weld_gate` (:306, called :3711) passes on 0.3 s within 1.5 cm at under 2 cm/s (`REJOIN_WELD_M`
   :92). **Logs:** the rod's end was 1.03-1.45 cm from the stub's centre at all 48 welds, first and second visits alike.
   **Inferred:** the gate takes the first passing run of samples, so it sits near the 1.5 cm edge.
3. **The command.** `_rejoin_send_attach` publishes `/drone_d/attach` (:3739), through the rejoin bridge
   (`graph_detach.py:223-230`) to the plugin. `src/drone_magnet` is not in this path: it owns QUAD4's weld. The rod end
   the gate reads comes from the rod's pose (`payload_mocap_emulator.py`, `/drone_d/magnet_tip_state`). **Logs:** it
   agrees with the tlog (R3405 #11: "1.3 cm" in the log, 1.28 cm in the tlog), so the reference is sound.
4. **The 1.3 cm is not resolved.** **Logs:** the distance from rod end to stub centre freezes at its pre-weld value for
   the rest of the spell, even through the crash (R3405: 1.28 then 1.27 cm, still 1.27 cm 0.5 s on, as the ring falls).
   The weld fixes the current relative pose, as the code already says (:92, `sim_magnet.py:11`). The rod now hinges
   about a line from the stub's centre to its top pivot, which misses the rod's own axis.
5. **The velocity.** **Binary:** `JointFeatures::AttachFixedJoint` compares the child's parent joint type with
   "FreeJoint" and calls `BodyNode::moveTo<WeldJoint>`. No named velocity call appears in its list. **Inferred, and the
   law above is the evidence:** the rod's free-body velocity stops being a state. Its velocity is now the stub's, and
   the drone's is the rod's plus the top hinge's stored rate. There is no impulse and no momentum exchange.
6. **The fold-in.** The joint's echo (`_rejoin_joint_cb` :3663) runs `_rejoin_fold` (:3746): the distance from pivot to
   plate point, clipped to 1.5 cm of the old length (:3754), becomes the slot's rod length (`_attach_ocp` :3308).
7. **The release.** `/drone_d/detach` (:1719). **Binary:** `DetachJoint` reads the child's spatial velocity and moves
   the rod and drone tree to a new FreeJoint. The rod keeps its velocity and the stub keeps its own, on the plate.

### Where the spin comes from

**Logs.** A rod with two ball joints can turn about the line through its two hinge centres, and nothing holds that
turn. The stub and the rod share it at release: the rod's rate just before the weld equals the stub's free rate within
0.003 rad/s (37 fold-ins with spin over 0.05 rad/s). A free stub coasts at one constant angular velocity in the world
(scatter at most 0.0009 rad/s, free spells of 36-228 s). So the `<damping>0.1` on the ball joint has no effect: with
an inertia of 1e-8 it would stop the stub in under a microsecond.

**Binary.** The BALL branch of `SDFFeatures::ConstructSdfJoint` calls neither `Joint::Axis` nor `JointAxis::Damping`.
The SCREW, REVOLUTE and UNIVERSAL branches read Damping.

**Inferred, and checked against the logs.** With the rod's end off the stub's centre by d, the rod's centre of mass is
off the hinge line by 0.733 d, so the rod is a pendulum about that line with gravity as its spring. Its inertia about
that line is tiny, so it is fast, and nothing damps it. Its rate cannot exceed twice its natural rate.

## 4. Could a real weld do it?

**No (Inferred from how the parts work).** No real flight has rejoined a carrier, and I did not read QUAD4's real
catches for this. The kick needs three things only the simulation has:

| The simulation | Real flight |
|---|---|
| A near-massless free link holding a large spin | The plate is fixed to the ring |
| A weld that replaces the arriving body's velocity | A catch conserves momentum, on a magnet that holds ~10 N |
| An undamped spin mode on two frictionless hinges | A cable has torsion and friction, and no such mode |

The magnet catches on contact, so there is no fixed 1.3 cm rigid offset either.

| Item | Carries to real flight? |
|---|---|
| The 3.3 m/s kick, the second-visit pattern, the 3.3 Hz spin | No |
| The rod reading of 1.001-1.009 m | No, it is this weld's chord, and this rejoin is simulation only |
| The tracker's 3 m/s envelope (`tracker_node.py:993`, fault line :1094) | Yes, and it did its job here |
| The rota, swap times, holds, path and height of the relay | Not touched by the kick, as far as the runs reached |
| Fold-in tilts in simulation tables | They carry this artefact, see below |

**For the thesis (Logs, then Inferred).** Every fold-in in the simulation has this step, at 0.00-0.51 m/s on a first
visit (spin up to 1.081 rad/s). X4's fold-in tilts show no trend for steps up to 0.43 m/s (0.85-1.18 deg), then 1.78 deg
at 0.58 m/s and 6.27 deg at 1.20 m/s. So first-visit tilts in X2, X3 and X3b are not flattered by it, and a step at a
fold-in should not be read as a measured transient. Worth a line in the known limits.

## 5. What to do

### Without a fix, X4 cannot pass

**Logs.** Of 8 second releases, 3 left a spin over the fault line (6.19, 7.13, 7.85 rad/s) and 4 over about 1.9 rad/s, a
1 m/s step. A step of 1.20 m/s already took the ring to 6.27 deg against bar R3's 5 deg. In the last 3 s before a second

**A. Universal plate hinge.** `generate_rigid_world.py:174-179`, behind a flag: `type="universal"`, axis tangent, axis2
z, damping 1e-4. The installed plugin reads Damping on this joint type (Binary). The stub stops on the ring within steps
and the rod has no spin mode (Inferred). Cost: a world SDF (2 relay worlds, Wesley's word) and a re-fly of X4 and the
fixed team on them. Risk: low to medium. The type is flown already on QUAD4's rod
(`x3_rig_drone3_magnet_L100.sdf:387-399`, damping 1e-4 and 2e-3), but not yet on a carrier, so a floor start must fly.

0.33 rad/s in the spells (Inferred). Do not keep the file's 0.1, which would come alive.

<joint name="payload_to_stub_{idx}" type="universal">
  <parent>payload::body</parent>

  <axis><xyz>{-sin a} {cos a} 0</xyz><dynamics><damping>0.0001</damping></dynamics></axis>

Here a is the plate's azimuth, and the stub's frame is world-aligned at spawn. The hinge's locked direction is then

Formula: 0.09 Hz, spin cap 1.17 rad/s, step cap 0.61 m/s. **Logs:** R3409 #9, a 0.58 m/s step, gave 1.78 deg. Cost: a
world SDF and the same re-fly. Risk: low, but the kick remains, capped, and the stub is still a free rotor.
