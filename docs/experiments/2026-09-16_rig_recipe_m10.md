# Card — tomorrow's rig takeoff recipe, in sim, at the rig's mass (2026-09-16)

## 1. Question
Does the OCP launch at its defaults (`start_taut` true, no creep, `lift_ramp_vel` 0.22,
`thrust_ratio` auto) lift a 1.0 kg disc from the floor with near-flat rods and hold a
level hover, i.e. is tomorrow's rig recipe sound before it is flown?

## 2. Hypothesis
Yes. R0276–R0279 lifted a 0.6 kg disc from the same geometry (rods at 5.7°) to 0.57 m
with 1–2° yaw excursion. The SIL bench with the solver prebuilt holds 1.0 kg at 0.589 m
(R0282) and 1.6 kg at 0.578 m (R0283), settled tilt < 0.3°, no throttle saturation. The
rig's 16:28 failure had `lift_ramp_vel` 0 and 1.6 kg told to the planner; the bunching
there was the OCP's references walking with the lift pinned to the floor.

## 3. The one variable
Payload mass 0.6 → 1.0 kg, in the world (`three_rigid_ground_m10.sdf`, generated with
`generate_rigid_world.py --payload-mass 1.0`, identical geometry to `three_rigid_ground.sdf`
apart from mass and annulus inertia) and in the planner (`load_mass: 1.0`).
Config `configs/experiments/ocp_hover_ground_m10.yaml`. Known mis-model kept on purpose:
the planner's `LOAD_IXX/IZZ` in `params.py` stay the 0.6 kg values, as they will on the rig.

## 4. Baseline
R0276/R0277 (0.6 kg, same launch defaults, seed-19 floor world): payload peak 0.574 m,
drone yaw excursions 0.5–1.7°, lift clean. SIL R0282 at 1.0 kg: settled 0.589 m.

Reviewer: absolute numbers verified; the claim as first worded **not supported** because the
0.6 kg comparison (R0276) is not a control: different world, a tracker parameter that has
since been deleted, and a post-LAND abort. Verdict on the recipe from the absolute bars:
lift, level, no abort **supported**, height **weak** (1.3 cm under the 0.55 bar, above the
0.50 falsifier).

Where the 6 cm goes, measured: every drone sits on its reference (vertical tracking error
0.0000 m in the hover window), kT is the derived 37.90, throttle mean 0.439 with no
saturation, and the fleet's thrust matches the thrust model to 0.1 %. The planner's own
drone reference is 0.8915 m at 1.0 kg against 0.9167 m at 0.6 kg: the OCP solution parks
the load below its 0.60 m target by an amount that grows with mass. Not a tracker or kT
effect; raising `thrust_ratio` would not move it. The SIL bench at the same mass settled
at 0.589 m (R0282), 5 cm higher than Gazebo, so the two plants disagree on this offset.

Rig rule that follows: read drone z against drone reference z in the tracker log. Drones
ON reference with the load low means the planner offset (leave kT alone); drones BELOW
reference means kT. `metrics.json`'s `payload_z_settled_m` averages from TAKEOFF + 5 s to
the end of the record, so on a hover-then-LAND run it spans the descent; use the hover
window.
