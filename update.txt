2. WHY
------

* ONE SOURCE OF NUMBERS. §9.2 says every number in the thesis comes from
  tools/metrics.py. run_experiment.py had its own inline metric code, so there
  were two implementations of the same quantities. Its metrics now come from
  metrics.summarise_run. plot_run.py computes no reported number of its own —
  a plot annotation is a number in the thesis.

* ONE LOADER, BOTH HARNESSES. metrics.load_run now reads the Gazebo run.csv as
  well as the bench sil.csv. They share a column schema, so one loader means
  compare_runs.py can put a bench run and a Gazebo run on the same axis — which
  is the entire reason the bench exists.

* THE PALETTE IS IMPORTED, NOT COPIED. plot_style pulls the per-drone colours
  straight out of controller_quad_load/rviz_config.py, and a test locks them
  together. A copied palette drifts, and then drone 2 is green in RViz and red
  in the figure you are describing in the same sentence (§6.2).

* MISSING IS NOT ZERO. Columns a Gazebo run cannot observe from outside the
  tracker (dN_acm, dN_tension, dN_elev_deg) are NaN by design. Those metrics are
  OMITTED rather than reported as nan, and those panels say so on the axes. A
  missing key means "this harness does not observe that"; a nan reads as "it was
  measured and came out undefined".

* REPEATS KEEP THEIR SPREAD. compare_runs groups repeats of a scenario and
  reports median [min-max], never a bare mean — a mean over five runs hides the
  one that diverged, and that one is usually the finding (§9.2).

* THE GATE NEEDED A STAGE THAT ACTUALLY RUNS THE NODES. Stages 1-5 are all
  offline; none of them can see a launch file that no longer comes up or a topic
  renamed on one side. Stage 6 flies carry_hover_n3 through the real launch.

7. A METRIC WAS WRONG — SWEEP WINDOW  ** read this one **
---------------------------------------------------------

Flying R0054 exposed a real defect in the metrics, not a cosmetic one.

`load_traj: circle` is ONE EASED SWEEP, not continuous laps. LoadTrajectory
._circle_theta stretches the duration so PEAK tangential speed equals traj_speed:
T = 1.875*2*pi*r/v, so ~9.8 s at r=0.5, v=0.6. On a 75 s run that is 2% of the
record; the other 98% is lift, hover and land.

Measured over the WHOLE run, the mean of the desired path is the HOVER POINT
rather than the circle's centre. radius_ratio then read 0.956 on R0054, whose
true sweep ratio is 0.811. A good-looking number that means nothing — and it
would have gone straight into a T1-vs-baseline comparison.

Fixed by adding a third §9.2 convention alongside the steady window:

    metrics.sweep_window(t, desired_xy)   the part of a run where the COMMANDED
                                          path is moving

Every trajectory metric now uses it — radius_ratio, phase_lag_s, stage_split, and
a new payload_rmse_sweep_m reported alongside the whole-run payload_rmse_m.
summarise_run reports sweep_window_s so a number can be checked against the
manoeuvre it claims to describe, and figure 06 draws and labels that window.

The mask is CONTIGUOUS by construction because the reference is published at
10 Hz and logged at 50 Hz: four of every five per-sample steps are exactly zero,
so a sample-wise test keeps a fifth of the sweep and inflates the apparent
commanded speed fivefold (2.99 m/s measured against a commanded 0.6).

Two tests pin it: one proves the numbers survive 60 s of hover padding either
side of a synthetic sweep, and that the whole-run figure flatters itself by more
than 0.1 without the guard; the other proves the staircase does not fragment the
window. Recorded in THESIS_PLAN §9.2.

Consequence: any circle number produced before this change is wrong. Nothing in
the thesis depends on one yet.


8. PART A STAGE V — THE PAPER'S ARCHITECTURE (velocity loop)
------------------------------------------------------------

WHY. Quan et al.'s Fig. 2 sends the dissipative controller's output as a VELOCITY
COMMAND straight to the autopilot. This repo instead makes it a position setpoint
for a 2 s horizon MPC — and R0054 measures that inserted stage as where the error
lives: the tracker stage alone is 0.869 radius ratio and +0.538 s of the +0.684 s
total lag. So the restructure removes a stage the reference architecture never had.
It is an experiment, not a bet: the paper asserts its architecture and never
compares it against an MPC-tracked alternative, so either result is a contribution.

THE ADAPTATION. Their autopilot accepts velocity natively. Betaflight accepts body
rates + throttle. The velocity->attitude->rate layer theirs provides internally is
ours to supply — document as an adaptation, not a deviation.

ADDED (design note first, per D11):
    docs/design/velocity_loop.md                        the design note
    src/controller_quad_load/.../velocity_loop.py       pure class, no ROS
    src/controller_quad_load/test/test_velocity_loop.py 17 tests (ladder step V-a)

    v_sp  = v_ref + kp_pos*(p_ref - p)     clamped to v_max
    e_v   = v_sp - v
    I    += e_v*dt                         clamped so |ki*I| <= a_i_max
    a_sp  = a_ff + kv*e_v + ki*I
    thr,q = tilt_quat_from_accel(a_sp, kT)
    w_cmd = k_att * quat_error(q_sp, q)    clamped
    stick = betaflight_rates_inv(w_cmd)

Two tests matter more than the rest, because velocity_loop.py REIMPLEMENTS code
rather than importing it (it has to stay importable without a compiled acados
solver, so the bench and the tests can drive it):

  * the rate curve is pinned against the CasADi expression in dynamics.py
  * the accel -> (throttle, quaternion) map is pinned against acados.py's version

A reimplementation that drifts from its original is exactly how thrust_quad_c went
stale at 203 and CABLE_LEN at 0.6.

ONE REAL DISCREPANCY, measured rather than assumed. dynamics.py, the SIL plant and
the Gazebo betaflight emulator all smooth the absolute value as sqrt(x*x + 1e-6)
because the solver needs a differentiable |x|. Real Betaflight does not, and nor
does this module. Measured over the full stick range the gap peaks at 9.0e-4 deg/s
(1.5e-4 relative) against a 670 deg/s span, and the centre-rate term is exact
everywhere because sgn*ax == x. A test feeds the stick this module produces into
the PLANT's curve and pins the round trip to 1e-3 deg/s.

V-b DONE, OFFLINE (tools/velocity_probe.py — new). Closes the velocity loop around
the SIL plant with no ROS and no acados: one free drone, the planner's own eased
circle r=0.5 v=0.6. Ladder bar is lag < 0.1 s.

    kp_pos  kv  ki | radius ratio  phase lag    rmse   max thr
       2.0 4.0 1.0 |       1.008     -0.014 s  0.029 m   0.339   <- defaults
       2.0 4.0 0.0 |       1.011     -0.009 s  0.142 m   0.338
       4.0 8.0 1.0 |       3.924     +2.252 s  3.128 m   0.600   UNSTABLE

Three things worth having: lag is ESSENTIALLY ZERO everywhere in the stable region
(the loop has no structural lag, where the MPC has 0.538 s); the integrator cuts
RMSE 4-5x (0.142 -> 0.029 m), which is direct support for the plan's claim that the
missing integral action is the problem; and there IS a stability boundary at high
gain (kp_pos=4, kv=8 blows the orbit out to 2-4x radius with throttle pinned at the
clamp). The probe flags saturated/blown-out rows as UNSTABLE so one is never read
as a good radius ratio.

  DO NOT over-read this. It is a FREE drone — no cable, no payload, no fleet — in a
  plant with no contact physics, and the MPC's 0.538 s was measured on a tethered
  3-drone carry in Gazebo. It shows the loop tracks; it does NOT show it beats the
  MPC. The control experiment (same plant, real tracker nodes) is V-c and has not
  been run.

WIRED (2026-08-06). `control_mode: 'mpc' | 'velocity'` on controller_mpc, plumbed
through dissipative_only_launch.py and three_attach_launch.py, DEFAULTING TO 'mpc'
so every verified path is byte-unchanged. The MPC path was touched in exactly one
way: the publish tail (takeoff spool, applied-throttle bookkeeping, last-good-command
fallback) was extracted into _publish_channels() and is now shared by both, so a mode
change cannot also change takeoff behaviour. Gate green after the refactor, including
the SIL smoke — that is the regression check that the MPC path still flies.

V-c RESULT: IT FLIES THE CARRY, IT CANNOT FLY THE LIFT.

  offline tethered probe (airborne taut start, control at the real 50 Hz):
      ratio 1.022, lag -0.011 s, rmse 0.036 m, peak drone tilt 33 deg   CLEAN

  SIL bench (starts on the stands, ARM -> TAKEOFF -> lift):
      R0067  kp2 kv4 ki1, no tilt limit   drone tilt 69 deg   envelope fault
      R0068  + 30 deg tilt limit          drone tilt 39.8, LOAD tilt 80.6  fault
      R0069  kp1 kv1.5 ki0.5              drone tilt 13.4, LOAD tilt 74.5  fault

Read that carefully. Adding the tilt limit and softening the gains fixed the DRONE
oscillation almost entirely (39.8 -> 13.4 deg, throttle peak 0.60 -> 0.44). The
PAYLOAD still capsizes. So it is not gain tuning and it is not the drone loop ringing.

Mechanism: the velocity loop has NO cable term anywhere in its feedback path. Each
drone independently servos its own position and nothing coordinates the three cable
tensions; the MPC path feeds a_cable into its model for exactly this reason. Through
the lift, where tension is large, changing and unevenly shared as the cone opens,
three independent position servos tilt the load over.

This is the SAME boundary the dissipative network already has, documented as
deliberate in dissipative_only_launch.py: "the pure decentralized network cannot break
the load off the ground ... OCP does creep + lift, the network does everything after."
The velocity loop inherits that limit rather than discovering a new one, which is
consistent with the architecture being replicated — Quan et al.'s is a transport
controller, not a takeoff controller.

So V-c is NOT "the velocity loop fails". It is "the velocity loop cannot own the
lift", and the fair test is the one the network already gets: engage it AFTER the
handover. That needs a mode that follows the network handover instead of being set for
the whole flight. Until that exists no Gazebo comparison is meaningful, so none was
run — a velocity-mode number measured through a lift it was never meant to fly would
be worse than no number.

ONE HARNESS LESSON. The first offline probe recomputed the command every 2 ms (500 Hz)
and reported the free-drone gains as comfortably stable. The real tracker runs at
50 Hz. That was fixed (control_hz, plant integrated separately) and it turned out NOT
to be the cause here — but the probe still does not model takeoff at all, which is
where the failure is. Same class of blind spot as the documented mini_plant one:
necessary, not sufficient.

9. STEP 1 OF THE OCP-HANDBACK PLAN: GEOMETRY AS A RUNTIME PARAMETER
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Supervisor's plan: use the dissipative network ONLY for the reconfiguration event,
then hand back to the OCP for the new fleet size, pre-compiling the OCP for n=2,3,4.
This is step 1 of that — and it is the enabling change, not the plumbing.

WHAT CHANGED. rho_i (attach points) and l_i (cable lengths) were BAKED into the
CasADi model, so any change of attachment layout meant a ~50 s acados rebuild. They
are now RUNTIME PARAMETERS (model.p = [q_ref(4), rho(3n), l(n)]). Masses and inertia
stay baked — they do not change in flight and keeping them out keeps p small.

WHY IT IS THE ENABLING CHANGE. A welded newcomer attaches WHEREVER ITS MAGNET LANDED,
not at a nominal ring point, and you cannot rebuild mid-flight. With geometry baked,
handing the fleet back to the OCP after an attach was impossible in principle. It is
now a parameter write: PlannerSolver.set_geometry(rho=..., cable_lengths=...).

Only n sets the problem dimensions, so ONE compiled solver per fleet size covers any
layout. Verified: n=2/3/4 all build and coexist (8.6 / 17.1 / 38.3 s), p = 12 / 16 / 20.

  src/controller_load_mpc/.../load_cable_dynamics.py   rho_p, l_p, geom_values()
  src/controller_load_mpc/.../planner_ocp.py           model.p, parameter_values
  src/controller_load_mpc/.../planner_solver.py        set_geometry(), per-solve p,
                                                       reference fns take p_geom
  src/controller_load_mpc/test/test_load_geometry_params.py   9 tests
  tools/prebuild_planner.py                            NEW, see the trap below

THE TEST THAT MATTERS. A model built on ring A, evaluated with ring B's parameters,
must equal a model built on ring B — any residual compile-time dependence shows up
there. Without it, a stale baked rho would make the solver plan against a fleet that
no longer exists while reporting perfectly healthy solves. A complement test proves
the geometry actually changes the dynamics, so the first cannot pass trivially.

A SIDE BENEFIT WORTH KNOWING: the solver cache signature no longer includes rho or
cable_len, so changing attach_radius or cable_len no longer costs a rebuild.

THE TRAP THIS TRIPPED, AND WHY tools/prebuild_planner.py EXISTS. The acados build
runs inside the planner node's __init__ (9-40 s). The SIL bench starts its scenario
clock on "poses are flowing", not on "the planner is ready", so after a model source
change it runs the whole run and SIGINTs the node mid-`make`. The interrupted build
has already DELETED the old .so and never writes the signature, so the next run
rebuilds too — forever. It presents as "the planner publishes no references"
(payload_rmse and track errors 'not present in this run'), not as "still compiling",
and it cost a gate cycle to spot. The gate's stage 6 caught it, which is exactly what
stage 6 is for.

  ./tools/prebuild_planner.py        # after touching the planner model, before benching

It builds the dynamics EXACTLY as planner_node does, because the cache signature
covers load mass / inertia / drone mass — a prebuild with guessed constants writes a
signature the node will not match, rebuilds anyway, and looks like it did nothing.
That happened once here too.

STATUS: step 1 done, gate green (146 tests). NOT done: n-keying the code dir so the
three solvers survive each other's rebuilds, the network -> OCP(n') hand-back, and
any flight test of a hand-back.

The sequence you asked for: MPC flies the trajectory, a drone detaches, the
trajectory HOLDS while the dissipative network redistributes, then the fleet goes
back to the MPC at the new size and the trajectory continues.

HOW TO RUN IT

    ./tools/prebuild_planner.py          # once, after any planner-model change
    ./tools/run_experiment.py configs/experiments/detach_handback_n4.yaml

  or by hand:
    cd simulation_assets && gz sim four_rigid_ground.sdf -v4 -r
    ros2 launch controller_quad_load rviz_quad_load_launch.py num_drones:=4 detach:=true
    ros2 launch controller_quad_load dissipative_launch.py num_drones:=4 \
         load_traj:=circle traj_speed:=0.6 traj_radius:=0.5 ocp_handback:=true
    #  ARM -> TAKEOFF -> (mid-sweep) DETACH button / ros2 topic pub /fleet/detach 3

  ocp_handback DEFAULTS FALSE, so every previously verified detach run is unchanged.

WHAT TO WATCH FOR, in order:
    [dissipative] DETACH drone 3 (slot 3); 3 drones remain on the load
    [dissipative] reconfiguring — trajectory HELD at t=... s
    [planner]     FLEET RESIZED to n=3; slot->drone [1, 2, 0]; attach azimuths
                  [90.0, 180.0, 0.0] deg
    [dissipative] HAND-BACK: OCP now flies n=3, trajectory resumes at t=...

NEW PARAMETERS (dissipative_launch.py and dissipative_only_launch.py)
    ocp_handback        false   master switch
    handback_settle_s   2.0     how long the fleet must be still
    handback_speed      0.12    m/s bar for every drone AND the load
    handback_pos_tol    0.35    gross sanity bound on load-vs-target
    handback_timeout_s  20.0    give up, resume on the network, say so

THREE BUGS FOUND BY FLYING IT, each with a specific cause:

1. THE DETACHED DRONE KILLED THE FLEET. After resize_fleet the OCP plans only for
   the drones still on the load, so drone 3 got no reference at all. A tracker with
   no reference is not silent -- it trips its own ref_stale envelope and disarms,
   which the manager reads as an unexpected disarm and aborts everyone. R0089:
   "ENVELOPE FAULT: reference stale by 1.15 s", 4 s after the hand-back. Departed
   drones are now parked on a hold reference at their pose.

2. THE ATTACH POINTS DO NOT MOVE — this is what step 1 was for. The pre-built OCP
   for n=3 carries a nominal EVEN 3-gon, but three survivors of a four-ring sit at
   0/90/180 deg, not 0/120/240. Handing it an even ring makes it solve for a payload
   whose cables are somewhere they are not, and the moment balance tips the load:
   R0093, payload tilt 62 deg about 9 s after the hand-back. Now the surviving subset
   of the ORIGINAL ring is passed through, which is only expressible because
   attachment geometry is a runtime parameter.

3. IT LATCHED THE WRONG TARGET HEIGHT. target_z was taken from the load's z at
   hand-back — i.e. mid-transient, after the reconfiguration dip. It latched 0.29 m
   instead of 0.60 and the OCP held that for the rest of the run (R0096). The
   commanded height does not change because a drone left.

AND ONE ABOUT THE TEST ITSELF: the first configs detached at t=30, which is AFTER
the eased sweep finishes (traj_t 9.90 of 9.82 s) — so there was nothing left to
resume and the hold/resume was invisible. Then t=24 landed in the lift transient.
Now t=27: mid-sweep, ~2.8 s left to resume, clear of the lift.

TWO THINGS THAT ARE NOT MINE BUT WILL BITE YOU

* THE n=4 CIRCLE LIFT TRANSIENT IS BIMODAL. Same config, same code: R0096 held
  0-4 deg payload tilt through the whole sweep; R0098 hit 43-51 deg at t=19-20 as
  the lift completed. The n=3 circle peaks at 16 deg over a whole run. Detaching
  from an already half-capsized payload tests nothing, so this variance sets a floor
  on how repeatable the demo can be until it is understood.

* THE RECONFIGURATION TRANSIENT IS SEVERE. While the network redistributes 4->3 the
  load dips from 0.50 m to as low as 0.07 m and tilts 24-30 deg. Pre-existing network
  behaviour, not introduced by the hand-back, but it is the thing most likely to be
  unacceptable on hardware.

* STARTUP POSE TIMEOUTS: now 8 of ~52 Gazebo runs. Every batch needs a spare repeat.

  THE DEFINITIVE RUNS ARE R0108 AND R0109: held mid-sweep (traj_t 5.1 / 5.3 of
  9.82 s), resized to n=3 on azimuths [90, 180, 0], handed back, and the trajectory
  visibly resumed -- the published reference xy freezes t=17-21 and moves again
  21.3-25.6. Minimum payload height after the detach 0.464 / 0.452 m, against 0.07 m
  (i.e. on the floor) before the two fixes. Worst per-drone error 0.32 m during the
  reconfiguration, 0.001 m once the OCP has it back.

  Earlier "full sequence" runs are weaker evidence: most held at traj_t ~9.9, i.e.
  after the sweep had already finished, so they never exercised the resume.

  THE TWO FIXES THAT MADE IT WORK, both found from Wesley watching it in the GUI:
    * _enter_planner_phase re-zeroes _ff_t, restarting the cable-tension feedforward
      soft-start. For FF_EASE_S the fleet is told to carry NO payload weight, so it
      under-thrusts and the load drops -- that was the payload hitting the ground on
      resume. Correct for a grounded creep start, wrong for an airborne hand-back.
    * The new solver had no warm start, so its first live solve was a cold
      reconverge -- _prime_solver's docstring already records that as "a jump and a
      scrambled MPC path". It is now primed twice from the measured state before the
      fleet is handed over, and the hand-back is ABORTED if priming does not converge.

RESULT OF THE EARLIER 3-REPEAT BATCH AT detach=27 s (R0100-R0102):

  R0100  FULL SEQUENCE. Held at traj_t=5.70, resized to n=3 on azimuths
         [90, 180, 0], handed back, TRAJECTORY RESUMED AND COMPLETED. Payload

  R0101  Hand-back TIMED OUT (fleet not still within 20 s) -> stayed on the network

         flew to LAND.
  R0102  Lost at t=8 s to a startup pose timeout, before takeoff finished.

So: 1 clean full sequence, 1 safe fallback, 1 lost to the harness. The capability
works; the repeatability is limited by things listed below, not by the mechanism.

TWO RESIDUALS WORTH CHASING NEXT

* ~18 deg STEADY PAYLOAD TILT after the hand-back, and ~12 cm of height sag. This is
  probably not a bug: three cables at 0/90/180 deg are NOT symmetric about the load's
  centre of mass, so a level payload needs unequal (moment-balanced) tensions -- the
  same geometric fact CLAUDE.md §8 records for the attach case. The OCP does solve
  for tensions, so the question is whether it is finding a tilted equilibrium it
  should not, or the only one that exists for that ring. Worth answering before
  quoting any tracking number from after a detach.

* LANDING WITH A DETACHED DRONE ABORTS. Both surviving runs aborted at or just after
  LAND (t=97.9 and 92.5), R0100 with 'drone tilt 71.4 deg'. The trajectory phase was
  already over, so it does not affect the result above, but the LAND path clearly
  does not expect a parked detached drone.

12. WHY THE ATTACH WAS NOT WORKING (2026-08-09)
================================================================================

Question asked: is the weld joint too rigid, and would an extra compliant segment
help? Answer: no -- the weld was never the problem, and the SDF weld is already
fully articulated. Three separate faults were stacked on top of each other, each
one hidden by the one before it.

Evidence base: R0031-R0046 (the 2026-08-05 re-flights, 12 welds) and R0116-R0118
(2026-08-09). Every number below is measured, not inferred.


12.1 FAULT A -- the handover cut the newcomer's motors  [FIXED]
---------------------------------------------------------------

12 welds in 12 runs, the same signature within 0.05 s of every weld:

    d3_thr  0.36 -> 0.000        (channel_2 = -1.0 exactly: armed-idle)
    d3_tilt 6 deg -> 89 deg      in 0.35 s
    ENVELOPE FAULT: drone tilt > 60 -> fleet abort
    the newcomer falls 1.1 m -> 0.10 m and lies on the floor

Mechanism. elrs_mux switches control authority to our tracker on the first
/magnet/object_attached. dissipative_node deliberately published NOTHING for a
reserved drone before the weld ("owned by the collaborator's approach
controller"), and it clears attach_pending on that SAME message, so its first
reference for the newcomer only goes out on the next 10 Hz plan tick. In that
window controller_mpc takes its `planner_ref_pos is None` branch and publishes
armed-idle -- throttle ZERO -- at 50 Hz. The mux had just switched a flying drone
onto that stream.

The tilt is a CONSEQUENCE, not a cause: payload_betaflight_comm mixes
`speeds = throttle +/- rate offsets` clipped at 0, so at throttle 0 only the
positive-offset motors spin. The drone loses lift and attitude authority in the
same instant.

This was already known to the SIL bench -- tools/sil/bench_node.py `_handed_over`
models the gap away as an explicit counterfactual and says in its docstring that
the shipped stack has the dead window. Nobody had closed it in the stack.

Fix, three parts:
  * dissipative_node._publish_pending_attach_refs() -- publish a hold-at-measured
    reference for every pending reserved drone, every plan tick, in every phase.
    The mux drops that stream until the weld, so it costs the approach controller
    nothing and makes the switch bumpless.
  * controller_mpc -- the no-reference branch now holds the last solved command
    when airborne instead of commanding motors off. Armed-idle is still correct
    on the ground.
  * elrs_mux / drone_magnet/handover_policy.py -- never hand authority to a stream
    that is not commanding thrust. Pure module, 13 tests, in the gate.

Measured after the fix (R0116): d3_thr stays 0.29-0.41 straight through the weld,
d3 tilt peaks at 10 deg, the newcomer holds 1.09 -> 1.02 m and tracks its
reference. The instant failure is gone.


12.2 FAULT B -- the approach drone rams tethered drone 0  [FIXED, unverified]
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

With A fixed, R0116 still aborted -- but 7 seconds EARLIER than anyone had been
looking, and before the weld.

    t=29.2  |d3 - d0| closes to 0.137 m
    t=29-36 it STAYS at 0.135-0.138 m while both drones translate 0.9 m in -x
    payload tilt   0 deg (t=29) -> 25 deg (t=35), all of it BEFORE the weld
    payload x      0.000 -> -0.53 m against a hover reference of x = 0

That is a sustained contact, not a near miss. The approach drone bulldozes drone 0
across the arena and the payload follows on its cable.

Geometry, and it is forced: the tethers sit at azimuth 0/120/240 and the gap-centre
weld target is at azimuth 180 (attach_x_offset -0.08), but three_attach.sdf spawned
the approach drone at x = +1.2 -- azimuth 0, directly behind drone 0. The straight
line from spawn to target passes through drone 0 by construction, and the config
runs with enable_obstacle_avoidance:=false (the launch default is true; it was
turned off because a large safety sphere seals the corridor and the weld never
happens).

Fix: spawn the approach drone at x = -1.2, on the gap bisector. It now descends the
120 deg gap between drones 1 and 2, clearing both by ~0.40 m, so the corridor is
clear with avoidance either on or off. One line in three_attach.sdf.

The post-weld payload capsize in R0116 (tilt 3 -> 62 deg in 0.85 s, abort at
weld+1.24 s) is downstream of this: the OCP had drifted 0.53 m by weld time, so the
OCP -> network handover snapped every drone's reference 0.3 m at once. Whether any
of that survives on a clean approach is the open question.


12.3 THE JOINT HYPOTHESIS -- what the SDF actually contains
------------------------------------------------------------

The shipped weld chain is

    payload =FIXED(DetachableJoint)= magnet_tip_link =BALL= magnet_arm =BALL= drone

and the ball is AT the weld point, so the connection transmits tension only. It is
kinematically identical to a tether

    payload =BALL= stub =FIXED= rod =BALL= drone

so there is very little rigidity left to remove. Two things follow, and the second
matters for the thesis:

  * "add a compliant segment" has little room to help. It is still worth measuring
    -- it never has been. git shows x3_drone3_magnet.sdf was created with
    arm_to_magnet_tip already a ball joint and touched exactly once since, to change
    motorConstant.
  * sub-claim N3 claims reconfiguration under a RIGID, MOMENT-TRANSMITTING joint.
    No world in the repo contains one. As it stands the simulator evidences the
    tension-only case that both reference papers already cover.

tools/make_weld_variants.py generates the one-factor ladder (each step changes one
thing, so a difference is attributable):

    rod    baseline topology, arm modelled as a proper slender rod. The shipped arm
           puts its whole 1 g at the link origin (the TOP) with an isotropic 2e-6,
           where the tether rods use m*L^2/12 = 2.083e-05 about their midpoint.
    seg2   'rod' plus a third ball joint mid-span (the extra-compliance hypothesis).
    rigid  'rod' with arm_to_magnet_tip FIXED -- the moment-transmitting weld N3
           describes. Expected to be the hard case.

Run:  python3 tools/make_weld_variants.py        # writes models + worlds
      python3 tools/make_weld_variants.py --check  # fails if stale
then point an experiment config at simulation_assets/three_attach_<variant>.sdf.


12.4 FAULT C -- the mocap watchdog is a wall clock in a 0.3x sim  [FIXED]
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pose-timeout disarms went from 0 in 17 runs (2026-08-05) to 6 in 5 runs, every one
of them ~1 s after ARM, and they destroyed a whole batch: in R0120 drones 1 and 2
disarmed at t=4.5 and never re-armed, so the run "welded" onto a payload already
lying at 57 deg on the ground.

Two causes, both fixed:

  * POSE_TIMEOUT_THRESHOLD is 0.25 s measured on the WALL clock (deliberately -- it
    is about real comms latency). Gazebo runs at ~0.3x realtime, so 0.25 s of wall
    is under four mocap periods and ordinary scheduling jitter reads as a dead link.
    Now `pose_timeout_s`, default 0.25 (hardware unchanged), 1.0 in
    three_attach_launch.py.
  * The warm reference of 12.1 made drone 3's tracker run a full acados solve for
    the whole 35 s approach, where before it returned early and never solved. It is
    now gated on the newcomer being within `attach_warm_radius` (1.0 m) of the load:
    the tracker only has to be live when the mux switches, and the weld needs 0.15 m
    proximity, so this leaves seconds of warm-up at a fraction of the cost.

Result: 0 pose timeouts in the next 4 runs, 3 of which reached the weld with a
healthy fleet. (One run still died on `reference stale by 1.17 s` -- the PLANNER
stalling, a different CPU-contention symptom. Do not run analysis while a batch
flies.)


12.5 WHAT IS ACTUALLY LEFT: the hand-out runaway
------------------------------------------------

With A, B and C fixed, three of four runs weld onto a clean, level payload
(pre-weld tilt 0.13-0.15 deg, payload x,y = 0.000, no drone within 0.43 m) and the
fleet then flies for TEN SECONDS. R0126 and R0128 are near-identical, one second
per row, t relative to the weld:

    t     payload_tilt  payload_z   d3_tilt   d3_err   d0_err
   -1        0.15         0.570       1.1     0.02     0.000
    0       38.3          0.555       1.5     0.10     0.131     <- weld step
    1       53.6          0.549       1.2     0.11     0.094
    2       54.2          0.560       0.9     0.12     0.065
    4       47.4          0.563       1.1     0.13     0.063     <- recovering
    6       41.0          0.562       2.3     0.17     0.058
    8       30.7          0.566      12.4     0.36     0.043
    9       55.5          0.556     135.1     1.14     0.534     <- newcomer tumbles
   10      180.0          0.214     125.8     1.25     1.055

Two distinct things, in this order:

  (1) A STEP AT THE WELD. Payload tilt goes 0.15 -> 38 deg in under a second. The
      cause is measured: the balanced-tension solve re-solves for four attach points
      the instant the newcomer joins, and the incumbent OPPOSITE the weld is asked
      to move in one tick --

          pre-weld  (OCP)      d0/d1/d2   horiz 0.460 m   elev 37.2-37.3 deg
          post-weld (network)  d0         horiz 0.343 m   elev 49.7 deg
                               d1/d2      horiz 0.476 m   elev 40 deg

      0.12 m inward and up for d0, in a step. `attach_handout` eases the NEWCOMER
      out to its slot over 12 s and `attach_ff_ramp_s` fades its cable FF over 1.5 s,
      but NOTHING eases the three incumbents. The soft hand-out is one-sided.

  (2) THE HAND-OUT RUNAWAY, at ~75% of the 12 s ramp. The payload is recovering
      steadily (54 -> 31 deg) and d0 is re-converging (0.13 -> 0.043) when the
      NEWCOMER loses it: d3_err creeps 0.10 -> 0.13 -> 0.17 -> 0.36 as it is eased
      out-and-down from its near-vertical weld pose toward the 45 deg rim, then
      tilts past 60 deg and tumbles.

  (2) is the failure the 2026-07-28 record describes ("drone 3's error grows REF
  0.30 -> ACT 1.06, |aCm| -> 20, tilt 10 -> 33 -> 72 deg"). It has been the stated
  open problem all along; it was simply never reachable, because faults A, B and C
  killed every run before the hand-out got past 25%.

Three candidate next steps, in the order I would try them:

  a. Ease the INCUMBENTS too -- ramp the whole network reference from the pre-weld
     geometry to the 4-drone balanced solution over the hand-out time, instead of
     stepping it. Removes (1) and gives (2) a level payload to start from.
  b. Slow or shorten the hand-out (attach_t_handout, attach_elev_deg). A steeper
     settle elevation keeps the newcomer near its weld pose -- the launch already
     documents this as the transit-runaway mitigation.
  c. Run the weld-variant ladder (12.3) against the HAND-OUT phase, which is where
     arm dynamics could plausibly matter and where they have never been measured.

(a) and (b) are design decisions, not bug fixes, so they are Wesley's call.

WHAT THIS SAYS

  * seg2 -- an extra compliant mid-span joint -- does NOT help. Survival 10.01 and
    10.30 s against rod's 10.39 and baseline's 10.08/10.15; steady tilt, d3 tracking
    error and d3 tilt all inside the run-to-run scatter. The hypothesis that the weld
    connection is too stiff is not supported.

  * seg2 DOES soften the immediate weld impulse: payload tilt at t+0.5 s falls from
    16-19 deg (baseline, rod) to 2-6 deg. Real, repeatable, and irrelevant to the
    outcome -- by t+2 s every variant is at the same 40-49 deg, because the dominant
    driver is the REFERENCE STEP of 12.5(1), not the joint.

  * THE FAILURE IS INVARIANT TO THE WELD MECHANICS. Four joint configurations produce
    the same trajectory: a step to ~45 deg, a slow recovery to ~30 deg, and the
    newcomer tumbling at t+9 s as the hand-out reaches ~75%%. That is the strongest
    available evidence that the joint is not the lever -- which is what the SDF already
    implied, since the shipped weld is a ball AT the weld point and so transmits
    tension only.

  * rigid (arm_to_magnet_tip FIXED, the configuration sub-claim N3 describes) is
    DECISIVELY WORSE, and this is the most thesis-relevant number in the section.
    Five welded runs (R0135, R0138-R0141):

        survival   1.76  1.87  1.68  1.91  1.84 s   -> median 1.84, spread 0.09
        tilt @+2s  89.8  97.3  83.0  97.2 106.4 deg -> median 97

    against 10.01-10.39 s and 40-49 deg for every ball-jointed variant. With a
    genuinely moment-transmitting weld the payload capsizes past 90 deg within two
    seconds, 5 times out of 5, and the newcomer is gone (d3 tracking error 1.09-1.17 m).

    So sub-claim N3 -- "reconfiguration under a rigid, moment-transmitting attachment"
    -- is NOT currently evidenced by anything in this repo, and it is not a tuning gap:
    the shipped worlds all model the weld as a ball AT the attach point, i.e. the
    tension-only case both reference papers already cover, and the moment-transmitting
    case defeats the current controller reproducibly. This needs a decision before
    examination: either restate N3 around what the rig physically does, or treat the
    rigid weld as the open control problem and solve it.

WHAT THIS COST, AND THE REMAINING LOSS MODES

  Weld rate was 4 of 9. Every non-welding run failed the same way and it is NOT the
  approach corridor: the approach drone never left the ground, logging
  `[approach] DISARMED - waiting for ARM on this node's command topic` for the whole
  run with `pose=ok` and a live external reference commanding z=1.0. The ARM on
  /fleet/command never reached approach_mpc_3. A startup message race, same family as
  the pose timeouts and the planner stall, and now the largest single source of wasted
  runs.
