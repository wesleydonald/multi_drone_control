1. Included geometry and omissions

In tools/standalone_dynamic_planner_cpp/src/ego_collision_model.cpp:
- assemblyComponents() creates a body box, then cable and magnet whenever SuspendedGeometry.enabled is true; payload is added only when payload_attached is true.
- cooperativeAssemblyComponents() creates the other vehicle's body/suspended components and adds attached_tether when its attachment geometry is enabled.
The ROS backend's suspendedGeometry() enables QUAD4's cable/magnet. objectAttachedCallback() toggles the extra payload component only if payload_collision_enabled is enabled. After release, that attached-payload component disappears; this mechanism does not automatically create a free dropped-object obstacle.
For the shared-carrier scene, refreshSharedCooperativeObstacle() explicitly disables a carrier's generic hanging cable/magnet/payload assembly and separately supplies its attached tether. So the carrier representation is its body box plus its assigned body-to-ring tether, not an additional free-hanging magnet assembly.

WorldSnapshot::timeIndexedObstacles() loops every ego component against every peer component, with names such as cooperative_drone_1::attached_tether_vs_ego_cable, ..._magnet and ..._payload. Cable-cable collision is explicitly a relation. This ego planner does not solve self-collision or carrier-carrier collision; it treats peer futures as given.

Ring/net are separately configurable in TransferBackendNode::refreshMovingBasketCollisionObstacles():
- segmented_ring: individual rim-bar obstacles, 24 by default, with measured ring rotation converted to conservative world AABBs; a translated committed ring-centre trajectory for each bar. NO net mesh, net bowl or filled interior in this branch.
- solid_box: one configured ring/net volume envelope (MovingBasketGeometry), not a resolved mesh.
- target_only: no ring/net collision body from this mechanism.
The local m2d_four_drone_sequential.launch.py selects segmented_ring, shared_trajectory and attached-tether checking. These settings must be confirmed in R4433's actual params file; the supplied fork diff alone does not prove every run used the local launch settings.
Plate positions define tether endpoints, but there is no separate arbitrary plate mesh beyond any containment provided by the configured rim bars. Rotor/airframe detail is represented only to the extent the body box encloses it. Slack, sag, elastic cable dynamics and future attitude variation are not explicitly simulated in this collision geometry.

2. Shapes, swing and tracking

makeCableComponent(): QUAD4's full bounded-swing cable envelope is a truncated cone approximation about world vertical. With cable length L, radius r and swing bound theta:
  top z = r, bottom z = -L-r;
  top lateral support = r;
  bottom lateral support = r+(L+r)*tan(theta).
Legacy uses a square section (pyramidal component); outer_octagon uses a circumscribed octagonal section. The polygon CONTAINS the admitted swing disk; it is not an inscribed polygon that cuts away real cable positions.

makeMagnetComponent() / makePayloadComponent(): nominal box, enlarged laterally by L*sin(theta), and upwards by L*(1-cos(theta)). Legacy uses a box around that set; octagonal mode retains the physical box Minkowski-summed with an outer octagonal lateral disk and the admitted vertical rise. The payload offset/half extents are configured; the name is not proof they enclose the actual object/funnel at every attitude.

makeAttachedTetherComponent(): a straight square-cross-section prism along the measured carrier-anchor to assigned ring-plate chord. It uses two perpendicular directions around that slanted axis, radius g.radius_m in both, and extends each end by radius along the axis. It is an enclosing box-like prism for a straight thick cable, not a world-axis-aligned box in convex mode. QUAD4's max_swing_angle_rad does NOT add a swing cone around this carrier tether; its orientation comes from measured endpoints and remains fixed in a world snapshot.

Important second difference between legacy_square and outer_octagon:
WorldSnapshot::timeIndexedObstacles() uses componentAabb() for every peer/ego pair in legacy mode, including replacing the slanted carrier tether by its large world AABB. In outer_octagon mode it retains actual component vertices and forms O + (-E), then adds tracking boxes. Thus the switch changes both QUAD4's envelope polygon and the preservation of the slanted carrier shape. It does not merely round one body box.
The independent checker in trajectory_safety_checker.cpp::checkIntervals() makes the same legacy-vs-convex pair distinction.

The ego and carrier tracking half-extents are added independently. If scalar values are 0.05 m and 0.06 m, the relative pair receives a 0.11 m half-extent inflation per axis, in addition to physical geometry/swing. These are assumed bounds, not bounds inferred from current motion by the separator. theta is configured, not estimated/proven/enforced by the collision checker. Carrier endpoint/shape/rotation uncertainty is not automatically covered merely because a centre tracking tube exists.

3. Prediction over a planning interval

Backend functions: cooperativePlateWorldPosition(), cooperativeAttachedTetherGeometry(), refreshSharedCooperativeObstacle(), refreshCooperativeWorld().
At refresh time tk:
  d_plate = measured_ring_pose * assigned_plate_location - measured_carrier_body_position;
  d_anchor = configured cooperative_tether_anchor_from_body.
A fixed relative tether prism is built between d_anchor and d_plate. For shared_trajectory mode its whole future is translated by that carrier's accepted committed BODY-centre trajectory p_j(t):
  anchor_pred(t) = p_j(t) + d_anchor
  plate_pred(t)  = p_j(t) + d_plate.
The plate endpoint is NOT independently recomputed from the future ring trajectory at each t in this implementation. This is a frozen relative-geometry/translation model. Body state, plate assignment and ring pose updates rebuild it; coalescing can delay materialization. Asynchronous/noisy measured endpoint differences alter its shape. The anchor offset is used as supplied; no future carrier-attitude rotation is applied here.
constant_velocity mode instead creates a finite body-centre prediction from measured position/velocity; that is a separate degraded/test mode.

trajectory_geometry.cpp::convexCurveIntervals() splits at knots/piece boundaries, obtains exact cubic Bezier subcurve control vertices, and convexCurveHullVertices() combines them into an enclosing centre hull H_j(I). A stopped committed tail can be continued at its stop point; a moving tail cannot be silently extrapolated indefinitely.
For each planning interval I and ego/peer component pair, WorldSnapshot forms:
  C_ij(I) = H_j(I) + O_j + (-E_i) + T_j + T_ego,
where + denotes Minkowski addition, E_i/O_j are relative component polytopes and T are tracking boxes. The ego body-centre MINVO hull must be strictly separable from C_ij(I).
The four configured spline intervals are not necessarily four seconds; their durations come from the current time allocation.

OctopusSearch::controlHullSeparable() prunes extensions; finalCheck() materializes separating planes for chosen intervals. Separator::solve() in separator.cpp uses a small GLPK feasibility LP, with independent returned-plane residual validation (and validated projection fast paths where used). trajectory_refinement.cpp::refineOctopusTrajectory() and appendFixedSeparatorInequalities() retain planes as QP corridor constraints, with the configured geometric inset and immutable prefix p/v/a.
TrajectorySafetyChecker::checkPiece()/checkCommitted(), via checkIntervals(), independently sweep actual ego/peer components over matching absolute intervals and test physical-space separation; RecedingHorizonPlanner::replan() checks prefix, candidate and configured backup/hold against the current world.

Swept hulls deliberately forget within-interval time correlation: ego at point A early and peer at A late can make whole-interval hulls overlap although they never occupy A together. Failure of separation means 'not certified by these hulls', not necessarily actual physical contact or global path impossibility. Optional exact temporal subdivision can reduce this conservatism without changing the motion; it remains OFF in the proposed line after the lab's unsuccessful isolated comparison. It does not repair an incorrect future.

Source chain:
- OctopusSearch::finalCheck() / terminalHoldViable() / finalDynamicsFeasible() distinguish a rejected padded partial's collision certificate, dynamic bound or terminal-hold requirement.
- RecedingHorizonPlanner::replan() rejects late candidates and revalidateRemainingIncumbent() checks the remaining commitment over a finite horizon. The result contains prefix_safety.obstacle_name and unsafe interval when a geometric check fails; coverage/time exceptions can also fail closed.
- backend requestExecutionFallback() -> activateExecutionFallback() tries makeBrakingHoverTrajectory() and checks it in the latest world. If that smooth brake is unsafe, it installs a measured-position stationary reference labeled UNCERTIFIED_HARD_HOVER, certified=false. This last-resort reference can be published even when it is not safe; it is not a successful safe-stopping certificate.
- A stopped position remains an obstacle to a non-yielding moving formation. The emergency layer has no separately certified 'ascend/retreat/coordinate fleet stop' escape. Normal replanning may find evasive partials, but no such candidate is guaranteed.
So the trace supports a failure to obtain/install a safe continuation followed by an explicitly uncertified emergency reference, NOT the claim that carrier cables were omitted or that a certified solver deliberately approved the crossing.
