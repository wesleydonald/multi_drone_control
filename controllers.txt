================================================================================
 CONTROLLERS EXPLAINED — MPC vs DISSIPATIVE, TAKEOFF, AND THE HANDOVER
================================================================================
 Multi-drone cable-suspended-load control (ROS 2 Humble + Gazebo/Ignition + acados)
 Written 2026-07-28. Scope: how the two reference-generation methods work, the
 files that matter, how takeoff works, and how we switch from one method to the
 other. Read section 1 first — the whole design falls out of it.


================================================================================
 1. THE BIG PICTURE — TWO LAYERS, ONE CONTRACT
================================================================================

The system is split into TWO layers that talk over ONE small message:

  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  REFERENCE GENERATOR  (one per fleet, 10 Hz)                         │
  │   - decides WHERE each drone should be, how hard it should push,     │
  │     and how hard its cable is pulling.                              │
  │   - TWO interchangeable implementations:                            │
  │       (A) centralized load-cable OCP     = "the MPC method"          │
  │       (B) dissipative spring-damper net  = "the dissipative method"  │
  └───────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                  │  /drone_{i}/reference_trajectory   (12 fields/node)
                  │  = the CONTRACT. See section 2.
                  ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  PER-DRONE TRACKER  (one per drone, 50 Hz)                           │
  │   - a small quadrotor MPC (acados) that FOLLOWS whatever reference   │
  │     is streamed to it and emits stick commands (roll/pitch/thr/yaw). │
  │   - IDENTICAL regardless of which generator produced the reference.  │
  │   - does NOT know or care whether (A) or (B) is upstream.            │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

The single most important idea in this codebase:

    We do NOT switch controllers on the drone. We switch the REFERENCE
    GENERATOR feeding an unchanged tracker.

The per-drone tracker (controller_quad_load) runs the whole flight. "MPC method"
and "dissipative method" are two ways of producing the SAME 12-field reference
stream it consumes. Swapping them is just swapping who publishes that stream —
which is why the transition is bumpless and needs no solver switch on the drones.


================================================================================
 2. THE CONTRACT — /drone_{i}/reference_trajectory
================================================================================

Every generator publishes, per drone, a Float64MultiArray of N+1 horizon nodes
(defined once in planner_node.py `_publish_ref`, wire format documented at the top
of planner_node.py):

    [ n_nodes, dt,
      px,py,pz,   <- desired POSITION            (world frame)
      vx,vy,vz,   <- desired VELOCITY
      ax,ay,az,   <- required specific-thrust ACCELERATION  a_ff = f_i / m_i
      cx,cy,cz,   <- cable-tension ACCELERATION  a_cable = t_i * s_i / m_i
      ... repeated per node ... ]

What each block is FOR (this is the key to understanding the tracker):

  * p, v      the trajectory to follow. Standard MPC tracking targets.
  * a_ff      the "how hard to push" feedforward. Its MAGNITUDE sets the tracker's
              throttle operating point; its DIRECTION sets the desired (yaw-free)
              tilt. For a loaded hover a_ff points up-and-OUTWARD and exceeds g,
              because each drone must support its share of the load AND lean out to
              keep the cable taut.
  * a_cable   the cable pull, as an acceleration, fed straight into the tracker's
              PREDICTION MODEL (see section 3). This is what stops the tracker's
              feedback from "fighting" the taut cable: the model already expects the
              inward-downward tug, so it plans thrust to counter it instead of
              treating it as a disturbance to reject after the fact.

Both generators fill in all four blocks. The dissipative net computes a_ff and
a_cable from cable geometry + a tension solve; the OCP gets them from its solved
horizon. The tracker cannot tell them apart.


================================================================================
 3. THE PER-DRONE TRACKER  (controller_quad_load)  — always running
================================================================================

Files:
  src/controller_quad_load/controller_quad_load/controller_mpc.py   (the node)
  src/controller_quad_load/controller_quad_load/dynamics.py         (the model)
  src/controller_quad_load/controller_quad_load/acados.py           (OCP wiring)

One node per drone (drone_id param), 50 Hz (FREQUENCY_HZ). It is a receding-horizon
quadrotor MPC with horizon N=20.

THE MODEL (dynamics.py, class QuadLoadDynamics)
  State x (17): position p(3), attitude quaternion q(4), velocity v(3), body rates
  r(3), and the four "actuator" channels u(4) = [roll, pitch, throttle, yaw]. The
  control is u_dot (how fast the sticks move), rate-limited (throttle slews ≤0.5/s)
  — that limit is the built-in soft-start.

  The one line that matters most (dynamics.py:118):

      v_dot = R(q)·[0,0, kT·throttle]   -   [0,0,g]   +   drag   +   a_cable
              └── body thrust rotated ──┘   gravity          └ the streamed
                  into the world                                cable feedforward ┘

  So a_cable enters the model as an OPEN-LOOP PARAMETER supplied per horizon node by
  the reference. The MPC plans thrust ASSUMING that cable pull. Betaflight "rates"
  curves map stick -> body-rate command (betaflight_rates / w_dynamics), so the
  model speaks the same language as the real flight controller.

  kT (thrust_ratio / est_params[0]) = assumed accel per unit throttle. Because the
  sim plant is really quadratic a(u)=c·u², kT is SCHEDULED to the operating point
  once airborne (_scheduled_kT): takeoff-safe low value near the stands, corrected
  to the true hover gain (~50) after the drone lifts AIRBORNE_MARGIN off its stand.

THE LOOP (controller_mpc.py `control_loop`, 50 Hz)
  1. Read the streamed reference (_planner_ref_callback parses the 12-field wire).
  2. Condition the cable term:
       - payload resting on the ground -> zero a_cable (a_cable=0 in the SAME model
         IS free-flight dynamics, so no separate "grounded" solver is needed; it
         re-enables itself when the load lifts).
       - cable_source="model" (default): trust the streamed a_cable open-loop,
         clamped to CABLE_ACCEL_CAP=6 m/s².  <-- SEE THE CAVEAT BELOW.
       - cable_source="measured" (optional): replace it with the IMU-derived cable
         accel, clamped+slew-limited (measured_cable_accel / _condition_measured_cable).
  3. Pin stage-0 state to the MEASURED pose + the ACTUALLY-APPLIED sticks (_applied_u),
     not the last solved sticks — so pre-TAKEOFF the model knows the throttle is
     really at zero and doesn't "leap" the instant TAKEOFF flips.
  4. Warm-start from last solve, solve the acados OCP, take stage-1's u as the command.
  5. Publish ELRSCommand(roll, pitch, throttle, yaw). During takeoff the throttle is
     eased on with a raised-cosine spool from a floor (TAKEOFF_SPOOL_FLOOR·u up to u)
     so the drones ease off the stands without a lurch.
  A bad solve is non-fatal: hold the last good command, reseed, disarm only after
  MAX_CONSEC_SOLVE_FAILS=15 in a row.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT — why the tracker is "feedforward-trusting" (this drives the
attach design): in the default cable_source="model" the tracker believes the
streamed a_cable, capped at 6 m/s², and has NO integrator. If the ACTUAL cable
force exceeds what the reference modelled (e.g. a newcomer being hauled fast
through a high-tension transient), the tracker UNDER-THRUSTS and the load droops /
runs away — it never integrates the error away. This is exactly why the mid-flight
ATTACH uses a slow "soft hand-out" (keep the newcomer near equilibrium so actual ≈
modelled force at all times). See the attach memory / soft-hand-out notes.


================================================================================
 4. THE "MPC METHOD" — CENTRALIZED LOAD-CABLE OCP  (controller_load_mpc)
================================================================================

This is the reference generator that treats the WHOLE coupled system — load + all
n cables + all n drones — as one optimal-control problem and solves it online. It
is the method from Sun et al. 2025 (noted at the top of planner_node.py).

Files:
  planner_node.py         the ROS node (class LoadPlanner): 10 Hz loop, phases,
                          takeoff, publishes the per-drone references.
  load_cable_dynamics.py  the coupled model (class LoadCableDynamics).
  planner_ocp.py          builds the acados OCP (cost + constraints).
  planner_solver.py       warm-start + solve wrapper (build_x_init, solve_horizon).
  reference_builder.py    turns the lift schedule + trajectory into per-node yref.
  creep_controller.py     the ground-to-taut takeoff creep (section 6).
  load_trajectory.py      the lateral load path (hover / line / circle / ...).
  geometry.py, params.py  attach-ring geometry, config.

THE COUPLED MODEL (load_cable_dynamics.py)
  One big state vector:
    LOAD_DIM=13   load position p(3), velocity v(3), attitude q(4), body rate w(3)
    CABLE_DIM=14  PER cable i: direction s(3), cable angular vel r(3), rd(3), rdd(3),
                  tension t(1), tension-rate td(1)
  Total nx = 13 + 14·n. The model captures how each cable's tension and swing couple
  into the load's 6-DOF motion — so the optimizer reasons about tension and swing
  directly, not just positions.

HOW IT RUNS (planner_node.py `_plan`, 10 Hz = PLANNER_HZ)
  1. Build x_init from MOCAP: measured load pose/twist, measured cable directions s_i
     and cable angular velocities r_i. Only the UNOBSERVABLE higher cable states
     (rd, rdd) and tensions (t, td) are resampled from the previous solution
     (observed_state_indices; paper Fig 8). Node 0 is pinned to the measured state.
  2. Advance the lift schedule (reference_builder.update): a smooth vertical ramp
     lift_z0 -> target_z (raised-cosine eased), then the lateral trajectory once the
     lift tops out, then descent on LAND.
  3. Solve the horizon (planner_solver.solve_horizon) — warm-started from last_X,
     hard reseed (COLD_ITERS) only on the first solve or after a failure.
  4. From the solved horizon, extract each drone's reference (position of the cable
     endpoint, velocity, the required specific thrust a_ff, and the cable accel
     a_cable = t_i·s_i/m_i) and publish the 12-field wire (_publish_refs).

In one sentence: the OCP method optimally coordinates tensions and drone thrusts
across the whole coupled load-cable system, and that solution becomes the reference
the trackers fly. It is what performs the TAKEOFF break-off (section 6) — the part
the dissipative net cannot do.


================================================================================
 5. THE "DISSIPATIVE METHOD" — VIRTUAL SPRING-DAMPER NETWORK  (controller_dissipative)
================================================================================

A decentralized alternative reference generator. Instead of solving an optimization,
it runs a tiny VIRTUAL PHYSICS SIMULATION — a spring-damper network of "virtual
nodes" — and reads each drone's reference off where its node settles. It is a port
of Quan et al. "Self-Organizing Aerial Swarm Robotics" (the MATLAB is in
matlab_dissipative_method/). Its whole reason to exist: a member LEAVING or JOINING
the fleet is its NATIVE operating mode — no solver switch, no hand-tuned transient.

Files:
  dissipative_network.py  the network physics (class DissipativeNetwork). Pure
                          numpy, no ROS — so it is unit-tested standalone.
  dissipative_node.py     the ROS node (class DissipativeController) — SUBCLASSES
                          the OCP LoadPlanner and reuses its takeoff (section 6/7).
  mini_plant.py           an offline rigid-body plant (drones+rods+payload) used to
                          close the loop in verification without Gazebo.
  verify_dissipative.py   offline closed-loop test harness (Tests A–J). Run:
                          python3 -m controller_dissipative.verify_dissipative

THE NETWORK (dissipative_network.py)
  Nodes: [payload, n robot nodes, anchor]. The payload node is PINNED to the desired
  load position p_des (the cone apex). The anchor node is pinned on the vertical axis
  a height cable_len·sin(elev) above p_des — the paper's fixed "table top". Together
  they pin every robot node onto an OUTWARD CONE of elevation `elev` (default 45°),
  so a reference can never tilt inward.

  Each robot node feels (step()):
    - a spring to the payload   (rest = cable_len)      -> sets cable distance
    - a spring to the anchor     (rest = cone radius)    -> pins it to the cone rim
    - a spring to a phased even-azimuth "slot"           -> pins absolute azimuth
    - springs to its two ring neighbours (rest = chord of the even m-gon for the
      CURRENT attached count m)                          -> even spacing
    - graph-Laplacian relative-velocity DAMPING          -> "the dissipation" that
      absorbs a detach/attach transient.
  With every spring at rest the desired cone is an exact force-free equilibrium; the
  damper makes it the STABLE one. Integrated with semi-implicit Euler in substeps.

READING THE REFERENCE (reference())
  p_ref = p_payload + cable_len · unit(node − p_payload)   (the paper's map: the node
  position IS the reference; the drone sits one cable-length out along the cable dir).
  Tension feedforward from the node's own elevation φ: t_i = m_load·g / (n'·sinφ),
  shared over the currently-attached count n' (so it RISES as members leave). Then
  a_cable = (t_i/m)·(−u) (inward-down), and a_ff = (0,0,g) − a_cable (outward, > g).

DETACH / ATTACH are one-liners on the flag:
  detach(k): attached[k]=False. Its springs/damping vanish, the ring re-closes, the
             even slots recompute for the smaller m, survivors re-space, and every
             remaining drone's tension share rises by itself. The freed drone flies
             away (fly_away_reference, a_cable=0).
  attach(k): the exact reverse — flip the flag true, seed the node bumplessly at the
             measured drone position, and the fleet re-spaces into an (m+1)-gon with
             each share dropping. For an OFF-CENTRE weld there are two extra modes:
               * balanced_tensions: solve per-drone tensions from a 6-DOF WRENCH
                 BALANCE at the true attach geometry, so an asymmetric attach set
                 holds the load LEVEL with unequal tensions.
               * SOFT HAND-OUT: a welded ring newcomer joins as a central lifter
                 (elevation 90°, over its own weld point) and is eased to its ring
                 slot over T_handout≈12 s, so its actual force tracks the modelled
                 feedforward the whole way and the feedforward-trusting tracker
                 (section 3 caveat) never under-thrusts. This is the robust attach fix.

Because the network only ever runs AIRBORNE (a taut hover), it never has to do the
hard ground break-off — that stays with the OCP. See section 7.


================================================================================
 6. HOW TAKEOFF WORKS  (creep -> OCP lift -> hover)
================================================================================

Takeoff is ALWAYS done by the centralized OCP (controller_load_mpc). Neither the
dissipative net nor an open-loop analytic lift can reliably break the load off the
ground from a shallow start — so the OCP owns it. Two sub-steps:

  PHASE 'creep'  (creep_controller.py)  — only for a GROUND start (start_taut=false,
    handover_elev_deg>0). The drones sit on stands; the cables are slack. The creep
    slowly sweeps the rigid rods upward (raising the drones on an arc) until the
    cables come TAUT at the handover elevation. During creep the OCP is kept "warm"
    on the live measured config (_prime_solver) WITHOUT publishing its horizon, so
    the moment of handover already has a converged warm start (no post-handover jump).
    An elevated start_taut world skips creep entirely and hands over immediately.

  Handover -> PHASE 'planner'  (_enter_planner_phase)
    Latch lift_z0 = current load height. From here the OCP runs the smooth vertical
    LIFT RAMP (raised-cosine eased 0 -> lift_ramp_vel -> 0) up to target_z, then the
    lateral trajectory, then hover. A soft-start clock (_ff_t) eases the cable
    feedforward in so the break-off is gentle.

The actual "pop off the stands" is a deliberate, subtle detail on the TRACKER side:
early in takeoff kT is pinned to a takeoff-safe value that slightly OVER-thrusts
(AIRBORNE_MARGIN in controller_mpc.py). That gentle over-thrust pops the drones off
their stands and tensions the cables — the load lifts — and once airborne the kT
schedule corrects to the true hover gain. Set the margin to zero and the drones just
hold at spawn, never tension the cables, and the load is abandoned on the floor. The
surge and the lift are the same event.

So the takeoff timeline is:
    creep (rods sweep up to taut)  ->  OCP lift ramp (load breaks off, rises)  ->
    OCP hover at target_z (+ optional lateral trajectory).


================================================================================
 7. HOW WE SWITCH — OCP  ->  DISSIPATIVE  (the handover)
================================================================================

The switch lives ENTIRELY in the reference generator. The dissipative ROS node
(DissipativeController) SUBCLASSES the OCP planner (LoadPlanner) and overrides the
10 Hz `_plan` to DISPATCH on a phase variable:

    phase 'creep'   -> inherited OCP creep         (takeoff, section 6)
    phase 'planner' -> inherited OCP lift + hover  (takeoff, section 6)
    phase 'network' -> THIS class's _network_plan  (dissipative hold/detach/attach)

So the SAME node does OCP takeoff first, then flips to the dissipative network in
the air. The flip (_enter_network_phase) happens on the first detach command (or an
explicit trigger) and is deliberately BUMPLESS:

  1. SEED the network from the current measured, airborne, taut drone positions
     (net.seed) — every virtual node starts exactly where its drone is, zero velocity,
     so the first network step makes no jump.
  2. Capture the load target the network will hold (_net_p_des / _net_hold_z).
  3. Stop solving the OCP. From now on _network_plan runs each tick: net.step(...),
     then net.reference(i) per drone, published on the SAME /drone_{i}/reference_
     trajectory wire.

The per-drone trackers (section 3) never notice: they keep receiving the same 12-field
messages at the same topic. Only the SOURCE of those numbers changed from "solved OCP
horizon" to "settled spring-damper node". That is the whole switch.

Direction of the switch:
  * We switch OCP -> dissipative (never back) once airborne, because the network's
    strength is mid-flight reconfiguration (detach/attach) and it must NOT attempt
    the ground break-off.
  * The reverse (dissipative -> OCP) is not needed: LAND in the network phase is
    handled directly by _network_plan (descend the held target to the floor, announce
    /fleet/landed to disarm) — see _fleet_command_cb / _network_plan.

Mental model:
  TAKEOFF  = OCP method (only it can break the load off the ground)
  HOLD / RECONFIGURE / DETACH / ATTACH  = dissipative method (its native mode)
  FOLLOW THE REFERENCE  = the unchanged per-drone tracker, the whole time.

================================================================================
 9. TL;DR
================================================================================

* There is ONE tracker per drone (controller_quad_load), a 50 Hz quadrotor MPC. It
  just follows a streamed reference and never changes. The cable pull enters ITS
  model as an open-loop feedforward, and it has no integrator — so it trusts the
  reference to be honest about force (this is why fast/off-model transients hurt).

* There are TWO reference generators that produce that stream:
    - "MPC method" = a centralized OCP over the whole load+cables system. It is
      optimal, coordinated, and is what performs TAKEOFF (the ground break-off).
    - "dissipative method" = a virtual spring-damper network. It is decentralized and
      makes DETACH/ATTACH/reconfiguration its native, transient-free operating mode.

* TAKEOFF: OCP creep (rods sweep to taut) -> OCP lift ramp (load breaks off) -> hover.

* SWITCH: one node (DissipativeController) does OCP takeoff, then flips to the
  dissipative network in the air by seeding the network from the current state and
  changing which code fills the SAME reference message. The trackers never notice.
  We go OCP -> dissipative once airborne and stay there through hold/detach/attach/land.
================================================================================


================================================================================
 10. LIMITATIONS & DEVIATIONS FROM THE PAPERS
================================================================================

Both methods are ADAPTATIONS, not verbatim re-implementations. The biggest reason
is architectural and applies to BOTH: the papers assume the controller commands the
vehicles directly (thrust / attitude). Our system flies real Betaflight flight
controllers, so we insert a SEPARATE per-drone Betaflight-rates MPC tracker (section
3) between the reference generator and the motors. That split is WHY the reference
carries the a_ff (specific-thrust) and a_cable (cable-accel) feedforwards at all — the
tracker has to be TOLD the operating point and the cable pull, because it does not
solve the coupled system itself. The rest of the deviations follow from real hardware,
rigid rods, and a hovering (not table-top) load.

10.1 DISSIPATIVE METHOD  (vs Quan et al., "Self-Organizing Aerial Swarm Robotics")
  * PAYLOAD NODE PINNED, not free. The paper couples the payload as a free node with
    gravity on it and drives it toward the trajectory. We PIN the payload node to the
    DESIRED load position p_des (the cone apex). The real load closes the loop
    physically through the rigid rods; building the cone on p_des (not the measured,
    lagging, possibly-grounded load) is what makes the reference LIFT the load instead
    of collapsing onto a sphere around it. (dissipative_network.py step()).
  * RIGID RODS, not cables. The sim attaches drones to the load with a Gazebo
    DetachableJoint (a rigid, moment-transmitting rod), not a slack cable. This changes
    what a "detach" and an off-centre "attach" do dynamically (a rod pushes AND pulls,
    and transmits moment), and is the root of the off-centre-attach tilt problems.
  * WE ADD A TENSION FEEDFORWARD the paper's tracker does not need (t_i =
    m_load g/(n' sinφ), and a_cable / a_ff derived from it) — because our tracker is a
    separate MPC (see the architectural note above).
  * ADDED: phased even-azimuth SLOT springs (k_slot) to pin ABSOLUTE rotation and kill
    the free-rotation degeneracy that destabilised n=2. Not in the paper.
  * ADDED: YAW-ONLY formation frame in balanced mode, so a load tilt cannot distort the
    references and self-amplify. Not in the paper.
  * ADDED: BALANCED (unequal, moment-balanced) force sharing via a 6-DOF wrench solve,
    for asymmetric / off-centre attach sets. Not in the paper.
  * ADDED: SOFT HAND-OUT continuation for mid-flight attach (central -> ring over
    ~12 s). Not in the paper; it exists specifically to work around the tracker's
    feedforward-trusting, no-integrator behaviour (10.3).
  * AIRBORNE-ONLY. The network is only ever engaged in a taut hover; it never does the
    ground break-off. Takeoff is delegated to the OCP (section 6). So the paper's
    "self-organizing from arbitrary states" is not exercised on the ground.

10.2 MPC METHOD  (vs Sun et al. 2025, the centralized load-cable OCP)
  * This one is a FAITHFUL implementation of the paper's online scheme, including the
    state-estimation shortcut from the paper's Fig 8: x_init is built from mocap (load
    pose/twist, cable directions s_i, cable angular velocities r_i all measured), and
    only the UNOBSERVABLE higher cable states (rd_i, rdd_i) and tensions (t_i, td_i) are
    resampled from the previous solution (observed_state_indices in load_cable_dynamics.py).
  * DEVIATION — it is a REFERENCE GENERATOR, not the final controller. The paper's OCP
    output would drive the vehicles; ours is tracked by the per-drone MPC (10 / section 3).
  * CENTRALIZED single solver for the whole fleet (one node solves all n cables). Fine in
    sim; a real deployment would want this on a reliable compute/comms path.
  * Geometry is baked to the world SDF (attach ring, cable length); a mismatch silently
    degrades the solve. It runs at 10 Hz.

10.3 PER-DRONE TRACKER LIMITATIONS  (controller_quad_load)  — these bite in practice
  * NO INTEGRATOR. Steady-state errors are not driven out. Under unmodelled drag or a
    force the reference did not predict, the drone settles with a persistent offset
    (e.g. a slight height droop) rather than correcting to zero.
  * FEEDFORWARD-TRUSTING + a_cable CAPPED at 6 m/s² (CABLE_ACCEL_CAP). The cable pull
    enters the model open-loop from the reference. If the ACTUAL cable force exceeds the
    modelled/capped value (a fast transit, a rigid off-centre weld), the drone
    UNDER-THRUSTS and can droop / run away. This is the exact mechanism behind the
    mid-flight attach runaway, and why the fix is reference-side (keep actual ≈ modelled).
  * kT (thrust gain) SCHEDULING is a hand-tuned hack for the sim's quadratic thrust plant
    (a takeoff-safe over-thrust near the stands, corrected once airborne). It is tuned to
    THIS sim; real hardware uses a different thrust_ratio.
  * cable_source="model" by default. A measured-cable (IMU f_ext) path exists but is off
    by default because the IMU->model->command->IMU loop needs careful filtering/clamping.

10.4 SYSTEM-LEVEL LIMITATIONS
  * OFFLINE VERIFICATION CANNOT REPRODUCE THE ATTACH RUNAWAY. The mini_plant tracker
    proxy is a PD controller; even made feedforward-dominant it still corrects position
    error in a way the real no-integrator MPC does not, so an instant off-centre weld
    RECOVERS offline (~8°) while it DIVERGED in Gazebo (12->90°). The offline gate (Tests
    A–J) therefore validates geometry, levelness, and the hand-out's near-equilibrium
    property — but NOT the runaway fix itself. That needs Gazebo.
  * OFF-CENTRE RING ATTACH IS SIMULATION-ONLY and, under a rigid weld, was the hard part;
    the soft hand-out is the current fix but is NOT yet Gazebo-validated. The stable,
    demonstrated fallback is the CENTRAL LIFTER (a centre weld adds pure vertical lift,
    no reconfiguration to destabilise).
  * NO OBSTACLE AVOIDANCE in the fleet reference generators. The single approach drone's
    planner (online_join_planner) has avoidance, but the OCP and the dissipative network
    do not — they assume clear space around the load.
  * DETACH/ATTACH validated in SIM ONLY so far; real-world tests are pending (section 11).
  * MINOR VIZ INCONSISTENCY: /payload/mpc_plan is a straight constant-velocity
    extrapolation (a cheap RViz shortcut in _publish_load_desired), so the payload's
    desired horizon draws straight even on a curved trajectory while the drones' horizons
    (taken from the real solved OCP states) curve. Cosmetic; the OCP's internal load
    horizon is curved.
